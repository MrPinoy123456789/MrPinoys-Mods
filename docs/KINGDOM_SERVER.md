# The kingdom server (d212ecae)

The owner's shipped Fabric server: Charter, Lemon, Foundry, Polymer, Geyser and Floodgate (Bedrock), plus worldgen
packs. Deploy with the `deploy-shipped-server` skill. The player facing handbook is the owner's Server Manual
artifact; this file is for agents.

Hardening (permission groups, LuckPerms, anti-xray, backups, admin runbook): `docs/SERVER_HARDENING.md`. Axiom is
installed and live (not disabled). "Operators" in Charter's rules below now means the `charter.bypass` and `charter.admin`
permission nodes, which fall back to operator level 2.

## Shape of the game

- Five kingdoms, created by ops only (`/charter admin faction create`) and led by a player an op appoints. Each is a
  race: Azrak (Dwarf), Aurelia (Human), Azurius (Merfolk), Faeloria (Fae), Everdelle (Fairy). A kingdom's keep is a
  permanent 9 by 9 chunk square that cannot be lost; the keep granary is off.
- Outposts are the only extra claims (overworld only). They hold points of interest and give fast travel; wars are
  fought between touching outposts. Players cannot found, dissolve or secede (`playerFounding` false).
- Charter publishes a read only map of held land for other mods: `charter/docs/AREAS_API.md`. Structures do not
  generate inside held land (`StructureMixin`).
- World: seed 3806707291501128365, border 10000 across centred on 0,0, the End off. Dungeons and Taverns, Structory
  and Incendium add structures. Pregeneration is partial; the rest generates on demand.

## Race weapons

Five weapons, three tiers each, in `foundry/examples/content/kingdoms`: `kingdoms:azrak_pick_N`,
`aurelia_oathblade_N`, `azurius_trident_N`, `faeloria_staff_N` and `everdelle_wand_N` (N is 1, 2, 3). All are generated by
`foundry/tools/gen_weapons.py`; never hand edit the JSON, change the script and re-run it. Upgrades are shapeless
recipes with the lower tier first, unlocked through generated advancements.

Rank 1 recipes (2026-11-03). A lost rank 1 weapon can be made again from plain materials, 4 items each so it fits the
inventory's 2 by 2 grid (Java and Bedrock):

| Weapon | Recipe |
|---|---|
| Runed War Pick | 2 iron ingots, a copper ingot and a cobbled deepslate |
| Oathblade | 2 iron ingots, a gold ingot and leather |
| Tide Trident | 2 iron ingots, kelp and an ink sac |
| Briarthorn Staff | a stick, an iron ingot, a vine and sweet berries |
| Everdelle Wand | a stick, an iron ingot, a feather and any small flower |

- The recipe carries `unlock_permission` (`race:<kingdom>`). Foundry (`RecipeUnlocks`) checks every online player every
  2 s: a player who holds the race node gets the recipe in their recipe book, the vanilla "New Recipes" toast on Java
  and a chat line ("Runed War Pick is in your recipe book. Craft: ...") that Bedrock shows too. Losing the race takes the
  recipe out of the book again. Operators are not given every race's recipe. Such a recipe gets no "holding the first
  ingredient" advancement, which would have shown every race's weapon to everyone holding an iron ingot.
- Each rank 1 weapon's lore has a "Craft:" line with the same text.
- Deploy the jar and the content together, in one restart. An older Foundry jar ignores `unlock_permission` and would
  unlock all five recipes for anyone holding an iron ingot or a stick.
- Ranks 2 and 3 are unchanged: each appears in the book when its player first holds the rank below.
- Crafting itself is not race locked, for any rank: anyone with the materials can craft a race weapon in a crafting
  table, but only its race can use it (`require_permission`). The recipe book just does not show it to other races.
- Upgrades take only the real lower rank. A plain base item (a stone pickaxe for the war pick, an iron sword, bone,
  breeze rod or blaze rod), or one renamed and dressed to look like the weapon, crafts nothing
  (`WeaponsGameTest.aPlainBaseItemCraftsNoUpgrade`). The server registers each weapon as its own item; the base item is
  only what clients are shown. On Java, Polymer sends the book the weapon's own name, look and lore. Bedrock is drawn by
  Geyser and may name the base item in the recipe (a "stone pickaxe" in the war pick upgrade); that is display only.

  do not. The sword is drawable (the bow pose, which slows a Java player) only while sneaking with Quickdraw ready, so a
  plain right click never poses; a held right click is one press, so the parry needs a new press once it is back. A

  dashes at full charge after a second. Holding sneak
  and right click shows a charge meter ("Quickdraw" and ten cells) on the action bar with a rising note; Bedrock players
  tap and wait, and always get the full charge after a second. The parry stops melee blows and projectiles only, never
  explosions, fire, falls, poison or magic, and answers only an attacker within 16 blocks (an arrow from further away is

- The Tide Trident no longer shoots a water jet on left click (it is a melee weapon, and its model is now long, like a

  |---|---|---|
  | Spellpower | I to III | 1 more damage for each level on everything the weapon strikes (bolts and areas) |

  second, the same share as unenchanted. Unbreaking and Mending do not apply: the weapons never wear out.

- Everdelle Wand rework (2026-11-03): it was the weakest weapon. The sparkle went from 3, 4, 5 to 4, 5, 6 damage (a
  tier III wand now does about 75 percent of a tier III melee weapon's damage a second, at 24 blocks). Mischief was a
  short single target bolt (8 blocks, 2 to 4 damage); it is now an area burst: the bolt flies 24 blocks and bursts on

  so a Fairy is never hurt or lifted by a Fairy's wand. (Charter's `kingdomFriendlyFire` already stopped the damage
  between kingdom members; the filter also stops the lift, which is an effect, not damage.) The engine side is new and
  general: `launch_bolt` takes `bursts`, and on_bolt_hit steps take `target "impact"` (see the add-foundry-mechanic skill).
- The Fairies' wand moved out of the demo namespace: `demo:fairy_wand`, `_2` and `_3` are now placeholders with
  `replaced_by` (a new item field), so a wand anyone still carries becomes the Everdelle Wand of the same tier the first
  tick it is in their inventory. Their old upgrade recipes are gone; on a server, delete
  `config/foundry/content/demo/recipes/fairy_wand_2.json` and `fairy_wand_3.json` (the deploy script uploads but never

- New mechanic actions and conditions (Foundry): `damage` and `knockback` take `target: area` with a `radius` (every
  living thing around the struck entity, or the caster, never the caster); `knockback` (`strength`, `lift`);
  `particle_shape` (`ring`, `burst`, `line`, `column`, `arc`, `spiral`, with a plain `particle` or a `colour` for dust);
  the `in_water` condition (in water or rain); and on `launch_bolt`, `model` (an item drawn riding the bolt, pointing the

  trident, pointed dripstone, spore blossom, nether star, feather) and reach Java players only, as Geyser cannot show

- 3D weapon models (`held_model`, a new item field): each weapon tier has its own model, drawn in the hand, on the
  ground, in frames and on shelves, with the flat icon as the fallback. `foundry/tools/gen_weapon_models.py` builds them

- Each weapon has `require_permission` `race:<kingdom>`. Charter answers that node for members (`RaceNodes`), through
  the Fabric permission API. Ops pass the lock. A race mod can grant the same nodes later.
- `keep_on_death` keeps the weapon with its owner. `handheld` makes the icon held like a tool.
- `tool` makes an item mine as a vanilla tool. The war pick mines as a stone, iron and diamond pickaxe by rank; a
  Foundry item without `tool` mines like a bare hand whatever its base item.
- Base items are bone (trident), blaze rod (staff), breeze rod (wand), stone pickaxe, iron sword. Never use `stick`:

- Bedrock: Geyser cannot read Polymer models. `foundry/tools/gen_bedrock.py` writes the Geyser mappings and the
  Bedrock pack; deploy with `--bedrock`. Geyser needs `enable-custom-content: true` (already set).

## Races and mercenaries

A player's race is a kingdom's race, stored on `PlayerRecord.race` and read with `CharterState.raceOf`. The weapons ask
`race:<kingdom>` and Charter answers from that race (`RaceNodes`), so a race outlives membership.

- A member gets their kingdom's race when they first join. Leaving does not change it.
- A mercenary is a player with no organization who has chosen a race with the Charter book (Become a mercenary) or
  `/charter merc <kingdom>` (`ChooseMercenaryRace`). They belong to no kingdom, hold no land and are killable anywhere.
  Grouping up is left to the players for now.
- A player cannot choose a race twice. Joining a kingdom that is not mixed makes them of its race (so a mercenary who
  is accepted elsewhere swaps race). Operators can also set it with the same action.
- Admin kingdoms (flag `adminKingdom`, for example Hey Dol Hollow) cannot be chosen as a mercenary race.
- The Mixed Race option (`Organization.mixedRace`, set with `/charter admin faction mixed <kingdom> <on>`) lets a
  kingdom take members who keep their own race. The Freedoms page says which a kingdom is. A leader's own switch for it
  is not built yet. Turning it off again does not change anyone already inside.
- Players see a race's own name, not its kingdom's: `Organization.raceName` (blank falls back to the kingdom name), set
  with `/charter admin faction racename <kingdom> <name>` (Azrak Dwarf, Aurelia Human, Azurius Merfolk, Faeloria Fae,
  Everdelle Fairy).
- Operators set a race with `/charter admin faction race <player> <race>` (any kingdom's race, members too, nothing else about
  the player changes) and remove it with `/charter admin faction race <player> none` (`SetRace`). A player with no race takes
  the race of the next kingdom they join, or chooses as a mercenary. Someone who has a race and no organization can be a
  mercenary of that race.
- Race passives: the `racePassives` knob in `charter.json` maps a race node path to effect ids and `RacePassives`
  refreshes them every 5 seconds for everyone who has the race, members and mercenaries alike. The default gives the
  Merfolk (Azurius) Water Breathing and Dolphin's Grace; the effects fade within seconds of losing the race. Other
  races have none yet; add entries such as `"faeloria": ["minecraft:night_vision"]` to the config to give them some.
  Entries read `[water:|swim:|under:]<effect id>[#amplifier]`: `water:` only while in water, `swim:` only while
  swimming (arms paddling), `under:` only while the head is under water; each is taken off the moment it stops
  applying. From 2026-11-03 the Merfolk have `swim:minecraft:dolphins_grace`, so they are fast swimming but walk the
  sea floor at land speed, and `under:minecraft:night_vision`, so they see in the dark under water. A `charter.json`
  that still lists plain `minecraft:dolphins_grace` keeps the old always-on effect until it is changed.
- Merfolk mine in the water exactly as fast as on land, floating or on the sea floor, and walk the sea floor at land
  speed (`RaceCombatPassives`). Floating or treading water, not swimming, they move about as with Depth Strider II;
  swimming is Dolphin's Grace's. An Aqua Affinity helmet or Depth Strider boots add nothing on top.
- Bedrock has no Dolphin's Grace effect, so `racePassivesBedrock` gives Bedrock players of a race extra effects on top of
  the normal list: by default Azurius gets `swim:minecraft:speed#1` (Speed II, only while swimming, no icon or
  particles). Entries read the same way. A Floodgate player is recognised by a UUID with a zero high
  half. Untested on a real Bedrock client: whether Bedrock swimming speed responds to Speed is the open question; if it
  does not, change the list in `charter.json` (for example drop it) with no new build.
- Planned, not designed: an altar that swaps a player's race.

## Locks on leaders

Two knobs in `charter.json` (both default true, set both false on the kingdom server): `leadersChangeGovernance` stops
a Lord or council changing the form of government (`SetSeats`), and `leadersAppointOffices` stops players seating anyone
in an office or making a new one (`Appoint`, `CreateOffice`). Operators and the members' elections still work, so a
vacant Lord seat can still be filled by vote or by `/charter admin faction leader`. The "Who decides" page then says the
server sets it and offers no buttons.

### Two Lords

A kingdom can have two Lords who rule together as equals. Operators choose them; players never can, and there are never
more than two. In the engine it is the Lord office with two seats in which either holder acts alone, and the book reads
it as "A lordship with two Lords", never as a council.

- `/charter admin faction coleader "<faction>" <player>` names a second Lord. The sitting Lord keeps the seat; the
  player joins the kingdom if they are in none (a member of another kingdom is refused). A third Lord is refused. With
  the seat empty it simply makes them the Lord.
- `/charter admin faction unlord "<faction>" <player>` removes one Lord, who stays a member. The other rules alone again
  (the office goes back to one seat). Removing the last Lord leaves the seat empty for an operator to fill.
- `/charter admin faction leader "<faction>" <player>` still means one Lord: it leaves exactly that player on one seat,
  so it also undoes two Lords.
- The same three are buttons on a kingdom's page in the operator menu (`/charter admin`).

**Either Lord alone** holds every Lord power: invite and admit, give powers, spend up to `lordSpendLimitMarks`, declare
war, move the Nexus, the Leader's Desk, the treasury in Charter Currency.

**Both are needed** to remove, expel or replace the other Lord, to change the seats or how the kingdom is ruled, or to
rename it. One Lord's attempt is put to the other, and the book says so ("Bob must agree"). Members still cannot vote a
Lord out.

**One Lord going** (leaving the kingdom, removed, or absent past `absentLordDays`) empties only their own seat: the
other rules alone on one seat and no election opens. Banishment does not touch membership or seats, for one Lord or two.

To give a kingdom two Lords on the live server (names in quotes when they have spaces):

```
/charter admin faction leader "Azrak" Alice
/charter admin faction coleader "Azrak" Bob
```

How the new traits work (Foundry, `config/foundry/race_traits.json`, built in defaults match the shipped example):
- Movement is server side, so Bedrock players get it: speed bonuses are movement speed attribute modifiers (a share of
  base speed, 0.15 is three quarters of Speed I), the Human mount bonus is on the mount's own speed and jump attributes
  and comes off when they get down, the Fae's half fall damage cuts the fall damage multiplier (a mixin), and the double
  jump and swim dash are velocity pushes. None of it touches the Fairy glide or the wings.
- The Fae fly by wing dash (`wing_dash` in `race_traits.json`; built 2026-11-02, see
  `docs/FAELORIA_FLIGHT_OPTIONS.md`): jump in the air beats the leaf wings, a burst the way they look (`forward` 1.2
  blocks a tick, `up` 0.8 looking straight up, half that looking level, none looking down). After a burst they fall
  slowly (a short, hidden Slow Falling, renewed while it lasts) until they land or press sneak. `charges` 3, all back on
  landing and one back every `refill` 30 ticks in the air, at least `gap` 8 ticks apart, so a Fae who paces the bursts
  stays up: at one burst per refill, looking about half up gains about a block a beat and looking level loses about
  1.7 (a `refill` of 20 holds height looking level). The server runs with allow-flight off, so a Fae who has beaten
  their wings is never counted as floating (`resetFlyingTicks`). Bursts never leave them faster than `max_speed` 1.3 across the ground, and only for a moment: a burst looking level carries about 13 blocks, then air drag takes it (a Fairy glides at up to 1.2 for as long as it stays up) and stop
  climbing above `ceiling` 12 blocks over the ground below. A hit from a mob, a player or a projectile takes a charge
  and drops them for `hit_drop` 20 ticks; poison, fire and falls do not. With no `wing_dash` block the Fae fly on these
  defaults; `"charges": 0` brings back the old double jump (`double_jump` cooldown in `worn_displays.json`, forward
  carry `jump_drift`). A burst resets the fall, so the half fall damage applies to whatever fall comes after it.
- A sense searches the blocks within its radius (1 to 16, knob `radius`) every two seconds (knob `every`), each player
  on their own beat, and points at the nearest find to that player only: a short trail of coloured dust from the player
  toward it (two blocks long at most, so it hints rather than marks) and an action bar line, "Ore sense: Diamond Ore,
  about 6 blocks away". Both reach Bedrock through Geyser. Nothing past the radius is ever revealed. Block lists take
  ids, ids with block state values (`minecraft:cocoa[age=2]`) and `#loot_chest`.
- Passives stay Charter's `racePassives` (unchanged). Every knob: `stride` (`bonus`, `ground`: underground, wild or
  anywhere), `mount` (`speed`, `jump`), `fall_damage`, `jump_drift`, `trade_discount`, `flight`, `sense` (`name`, `blocks`,
  `radius`, `every`, `colour`). A race listed in the file replaces its built in traits.
- Better trades (`trade_discount`, Humans 0.1, at most 0.5): when a villager or wandering trader starts trading with
  the player, each offer's first cost drops by that share, rounded (a 10 emerald offer costs 9, a 3 emerald one stays
  3), never below one; on the server, the same per offer change vanilla uses for Hero of the Village, with no effect or
  particles. It comes off when trading stops. Charter Currency's `/shop` is not discounted: its prices are flat per
  listing with no per player hook, and it does not know races. Humans no longer get Luck (Charter's default
  `racePassives` no longer lists `aurelia`; a server whose `charter.json` still lists it must remove that entry).
- Fairy flight (`flight`, Fairies only): sneak and jump on the ground springs them about 7 blocks up and starts the glide
  at the top (`leap` 1.05, `leap_cooldown` 100 ticks). While gliding, jump beats the wings: the fall is cancelled, they
  lift (`beat_lift` 0.55) and are pushed the way they look (`beat_push` 0.3), never past 1.2 blocks a tick across the
  ground (`max_speed`; a rocket goes about 1.7). Three beats (`beats`); all come back after half a second on the ground,
  one every 10 s in the air (`refill` 200). The action bar shows the beats left. It is the middle way between gliding
  only and rockets: a Fairy can get airborne and climb out of a dive, but cannot stay up for ever or keep pace with
  rockets. Rockets still work for them as for anyone. Unverified: whether a Bedrock client reports a jump press while
  gliding (if not, Bedrock Fairies get the leap but not the beats).
- Sense lists also take `#loot:<prefix>`: a container that still has its loot to roll whose loot table id starts with
  the prefix (the Humans' list is `minecraft:chests/village/`, `shipwreck_`, `buried_treasure`, `abandoned_mineshaft` and
  `ruined_portal`; no ancient cities, end cities, bastions or trial vaults). Ancient debris is nobody's to sense.
- The Human look is now a sashimono: the pole against the back, an arm from its top reaching back, and the tall black
  cloth hanging from it beside the pole, so it shows full on from the left and right; three silver bands across its top,
  the crowned skull below, silver rings where it is laced to the pole.

## The Admin Book and operator commands

Operators get an **Admin Book** (a red book with a glint) when they join or respawn without one, in a free hotbar slot
other than slot 9 (the Charter book's). `opsGetAdminBook` in `charter.json` turns that off; `/charter admin give
adminbook [player]` hands one out. Using it opens the operators' menu, the same as `/charter admin` on its own. Only an
operator may hold one: anyone else's is taken away within a second, it vanishes on death instead of dropping, and an
operator keeps one copy. Every press in the menu checks again that the player is still an operator.

The menu is laid out by what an operator looks up:

- **Players:** who is online (and a search for anyone the server has seen). A player's page shows their kingdom,
  race, offices, whether they are held or banished, and when they were last seen, with Go to them, Bring them here, put
  in or take out of a kingdom, the three Lord actions, set race, and give either book.
- **Kingdoms:** each kingdom's page shows its Lords, Nexus, race and flags, with Go to the Nexus, members (each opens
  their player page), add a member, the Lord actions, rename, race name, mixed race, admin kingdom, claim bonus, move
  the keep here and dissolve.
- **Keeps and land:** every keep's exact Nexus block with a Go button, the land map, supply lines and granary links.
- **Votes:** every open vote; pick one to count it now. Also raider sites, new kingdom, server tools and help.

Anything that cannot be undone (taking a member out, moving a keep, counting a vote, dissolving) asks first. The menu
passes players by UUID and kingdoms by id, so two players with one name are never confused.

Typed, under `/charter admin` (`/charter admin help` prints them all):

- `kingdoms`: each kingdom with its Lords, members and Nexus. `keeps`: every keep's Nexus block, with a Go link.
- `faction ranks <faction>`: its ranks, top first: how many hold each, their names (the first 12), and the powers
  each carries. The menu has the same on each kingdom's page ("Ranks"), and its member list shows each member's rank.
  `player <player>` and the menu's player page also say the player's rank and what it may do.
- `goto <faction>`: go to a kingdom's Nexus, in any dimension. `player <player>`: the facts on the player's page.
- `votes`: open votes with their numbers and a Close now link. `vote close <number>` now suggests the numbers.
- `faction remove <faction> <player>`: take a member out (a Lord loses the seat too).
  `faction rename <faction> <name>`: a new name; its land, members and history stay.
- `faction nexus <faction> [<x> <y> <z>]`: move that kingdom's Nexus lodestone to your feet, or to the coordinates, inside
  its own keep's nine chunks (the same rules as `/charter nexus move`: not outside the keep, not while it is open to attack,
  not for an NPC kingdom). The keep, its land, teleport and jail follow; a lift under way is ended and the carrier loses the
  item. The Chronicle records "the Nexus of <kingdom> was moved by an operator". From the console, give coordinates. The
  menu has "Move its Nexus here" on each players' kingdom's page.
- Every `<player>` takes an online name, a UUID, or the name of anyone the server has seen. Every `<faction>` takes a
  name (suggested as you type) or an id in quotes (`"org:..."`). No command was renamed or removed.
- `moderation`: who is muted, barred from the land or holding Ledger access now, then the last 15 Lord moderation and
  Ledger events with their reasons. `ledger grant|revoke <player>`, `ledger status`: see "Ledger access for a Lord"
  below. The menu has the moderation log under Tools ("What Lords did"), and a Lord's player page has "Ledger access,
  1 hour" (or "Take back Ledger access" while one stands).

## The admin kingdom pass

`adminKingdomBypass` (top level of `charter.json`, default `true`). Every member of an admin kingdom (Mossbeard's
Muddle on the live server), at any rank, has the operator pass (`charter.bypass`) without LuckPerms, so they can do admin
work in other kingdoms. It comes from real membership only: standing on the admin kingdom's land gives nothing, and
leaving or being taken out ends it at once (it is checked at the moment of every action, never stored). Operators and
LuckPerms holders keep their pass exactly as before.

With it a member of the admin kingdom:

- builds, breaks and uses things (doors, chests, levers, entities) on any kingdom's claims, the keep core included,
  which even an operator's pass does not do;
- walks into closed keeps;
- moves or lifts any kingdom's Nexus;
- is exempt from every spawn rule, as an operator is: they build in the spawn, and their hits, arrows and TNT hurt
  players and mobs there;
- is never held to the `requireAffiliation` look only rule (they are in a kingdom anyway).

It does not give `charter.admin` (the `/charter admin` commands, the operators' menu and the Admin Book stay for
operators and LuckPerms admins). It does not make them immune to their own Lord: the admin kingdom's Lord still mutes,
bars and kicks its members, and no other kingdom's Lord can reach them anyway. Friendly fire is unchanged: they cannot
hurt their own kingdom's members, and they hurt other kingdoms' members outside the spawn as anyone does. Off, an admin
kingdom's members are held to the spawn rules and every kingdom's claims like anyone else.

Test: `AdminKingdomBypassGameTest`.

### Godmode for the admin kingdom

`adminKingdomGodmode` (top level of `charter.json`, default `true`). Every member of an admin kingdom, at any rank, takes
no damage at all: not from players, mobs, falls, fire, lava, drowning or starving. Only what ignores invulnerability in
vanilla gets through, `/kill` and falling out of the world, so a stuck staffer can still respawn. Like the pass it comes
from real membership only, never from standing on the admin kingdom's land, and leaving or being taken out ends it at
once. Off, they are hurt like anyone else. Code: `AdminGodmode`. Test: `AdminGodmodeGameTest`.

## Lords discipline their own citizens (2026-11-02)

Kingdom Lords are the moderators of this server; there is no moderator group. A Lord's power stops at their own
kingdom: they can act only on a member of the kingdom they rule (a member of one of its sub-factions counts), never on
a member of another kingdom, a player in no kingdom, another Lord (two Lords cannot moderate each other), themselves,
or anyone holding `charter.admin` or `charter.bypass`. Lords get nothing from BanHammer, Ledger or vanilla: these are
Charter commands, checked by Charter on the server. Code: `charter/core/src/main/java/charter/moderation/` (the rules)
and `charter/fabric/src/main/java/charter/mc/cmd/LordCommands.java`, `mc/moderation/` (enforcement).

In the Charter book, a Lord's Leader's Desk has **Discipline citizens**, so a Lord on Bedrock or a controller never types:

- **Discipline** shows who is held now (muted or barred from the land, with the time left), then the citizens a Lord can
  act on: held citizens first, then everyone else online. Operators, other Lords and the Lord themselves are not
  listed. A Lord who also judges finds **Prisoners and justice** here; someone who judges but is no Lord keeps the
  Justice button on their desk instead, so neither path costs an extra press.
- Pick a citizen, then **Mute**, **Bar from land** or **Kick**, or lift a hold with **Unmute** or **Lift the bar**. A
  citizen who is offline can only have a hold lifted.
- A time from presets within the cap (mute: 5, 10, 30 minutes, 1 hour; suspend: 1, 3, 6, 12 hours; the cap itself is
  offered when a lower knob cuts the presets), then a reason: five common ones in one press (spamming the chat, rude
  or hateful words, griefing or stealing, harassing a player, ignoring warnings), or **Another reason** for a text box.
- Presses from the book in hand: mute or bar from land 7, kick 6, unmute or lift the bar 5. The result lands on
  Discipline in green, or the refusal on the citizen in red.
- The book and the commands run one path (`LordActions.run`), so the operator check, the online check, the rules and
  the word to the operators are the same either way.

Typed, the same powers:

| Command | What it does | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| `/charter lord` | what a Lord can do, and which of their citizens are held now | |
| `/charter lord mute <player> <time> <reason>` | mutes the citizen: stops their chat, `/msg`, `/tell`, `/me` (anything that sends a signed message); `/charter` and reading chat still work | at most 1 hour (`lordMuteMaxMinutes`, default 60) |
| `/charter lord unmute <player>` | lets them speak again | |
| `/charter lord suspend <player> <time> <reason>` | bars the citizen from the kingdom's land with the banishment push; they stay a member with their rank, and everywhere else is open | at most 12 hours (`lordSuspendMaxHours`, default 12) |
| `/charter lord unsuspend <player>` | lets them back onto the land | |
| `/charter lord kick <player> <reason>` | disconnects the citizen with the Lord's name and reason; nothing is banned and they may log straight back in | |

- Times read as `10m`, `2h`, `1h30m`. A time over the cap is refused (not cut down) and the refusal names the cap. A
  new mute or suspension replaces the old one from now; it never adds to it.
- A reason of at least 3 characters is required (cut to 80). The citizen is told who did it, for how long and why.
- Mute, suspend and kick need the citizen online, because only then can Charter ask the permissions mod whether they
  are an operator. Unmute and unsuspend work offline.
- Operators are asked again at enforcement: someone made an operator after being muted or suspended is let go on their
  next message or step, and the hold is dropped.
- Every action is a history event (`ModerationEvents.*` in `history.jsonl`, with the reason), a public Chronicle line
  without the reason ("Ann of Azrak muted Bob for 10 minutes."), and a line to every operator online and the
  console (`[Lords] Ann of Azrak muted Bob for 10 minutes: spam`). `/charter admin moderation` and a player's page
  show what is in force.
- Only the kingdom that suspended a player can lift it or give a new one while it runs, even after the player has
  joined another kingdom.

### Ledger access for a Lord (one hour, operators only)

`/charter admin ledger grant <player>` gives a **current Lord** read only Ledger for exactly one hour, so they can look
into trouble on an operator's behalf. It runs, as the server console, `lp user <uuid> permission unsettemp <node>`
(clearing anything set by hand) and then `lp user <uuid> permission settemp <node> true 1h replace` for each node, with the nodes `ledger.commands.root`,
`ledger.commands.inspect`, `ledger.commands.search` and `ledger.commands.status`. Never rollback, restore, preview,
teleport, player or purge. The operator is told the expiry time (UTC); the Lord is told (or gets it as mail when
offline). A second grant while one stands is refused, so it cannot be rolled forward: when the hour is up the access
lapses (LuckPerms drops the nodes; Charter clears its record and unsets them as a safeguard) and an operator must grant
it again. `ledger revoke <player>` ends it at once, and it is taken back automatically within five seconds if the
player stops being a Lord. `ledger status` lists who holds it. Without LuckPerms installed the command says so and
changes nothing. Grants and their ends are admin events, kept out of the public Chronicle.

**Ledger itself cannot limit a Lord's lookups to their own kingdom.** For that hour the Lord can inspect and search the
whole server's block history, every kingdom included. A grant is an operator's trust decision for one hour, not a
kingdom scoped power. LuckPerms must already know the player, so they must have joined at least once.

## Race trinkets: opening the screen and how they look

Four race trinkets (Tidal Gills, Forgeman's Pauldron, Kingdom Cape, Antler Circlet) sit in Trinkets Updated slots; the
Fairy Wings are an elytra in the chest slot and show on every client.

- **Opening the Trinkets screen.** Any player, op or not, can use `/trinkets` (the patch registers it with no permission
  check), `/trinkets compact` to switch between the compact and flat layouts, or right click a helmet, chestplate,
  leggings or boots slot in the inventory. `/charter trinkets` runs the same `/trinkets` as the player, and the Charter
  book home has a Trinkets button. Both need no op level. A permission mod that restricts commands would also have to
  allow `trinkets`.
- **What other players see.** A vanilla Java client cannot render Trinkets models (the patch README says trinkets
  "won't render visually"; a resource pack alone cannot draw them). So Foundry draws them itself (`WornDisplays`): one
  Polymer item display per worn Foundry trinket, turned with the body (cape, pauldron) or head (circlet, gills). No
  client mod is needed, only Trinkets on the server. It shows the item's own model (a flat sprite today), appears
  while the trinket is in its slot, and goes on unequip, death, logout, a new player object or a dimension change.
  It is hidden while the player is invisible, swimming, gliding or asleep. Worn trinkets are looked for every 10
  ticks and the per tick turning runs only while someone wears one. The gills are hidden from their own wearer (they
  sit in front of the eyes); the rest show to everyone including the wearer in third person.
- **Tuning it live.** `config/foundry/worn_displays.json` (shipped example: `foundry/examples/config/worn_displays.json`)
  sets `up`, `back`, `side` (blocks from the feet; back is behind, side is to the player's right), `scale`,
  `on_head`, `rotation` [x, y, z] degrees and `hide_from_wearer`, per slot under `slots` and per item under `items`
  (an item entry names its `slot` and inherits it). Any field can be left out. Foundry checks the file every two
  seconds and re-places everyone's displays when it changes, with no restart; a file with a problem logs a warning and
  the last good placements stay. Without the file the built in defaults apply.
  Unverified on a live client: how each item looks and sits, size and facing, and cost with many players. A client with
  the Trinkets mod also gets its own render from `foundry/examples/assets/kingdoms/trinkets/` and may show a second
  copy; delete those files if so.
- **Why the wearer saw nothing at first, and the check command.** Polymer sends an attached display only to players
  tracking the entity, and nobody tracks themselves, so the wearer never got their own displays (a lone player saw
  nothing in F5). `WornDisplays` now adds the wearer by hand, except for a placement with `hide_from_wearer`. Each
  display is also placed before it is attached, so the first packet already has its size and position. `/foundry worn
  list [player]` (op) prints each player's worn Foundry trinkets, the displays made for them, who receives each one and
  where it sits: no display line means "not created" (no placement for that slot, or Trinkets absent); a line with the
  right watchers but nothing on screen means "created but not visible" (try larger `up`, `back`, `side` or `scale`).
  The harness cannot make mock players track each other, so Polymer's real tracking of other players is unverified.
- **No lag.** Two settings in `worn_displays.json`, per slot or item. `head_slot` (default on for `head/hat`, the
  circlet) sends the item to other players as if it were in the helmet slot (a per viewer equipment packet), so it
  follows the head exactly; the real helmet slot is never changed, the wearer is not sent it, and it falls back to a
  display while a real helmet is worn and returns when it comes off. `ride` (default on for the displays) mounts the
  display on the player as a virtual passenger, so the client moves it every frame instead of waiting for position
  packets; heights are then measured from the player's top, which Foundry handles. Set either to false to go back to the
  old behaviour if something looks wrong. Both are unverified on a live client.
- **What the wearer sees (`/trinketview`).** By default you do not see your own trinkets; everyone else does. The client
  draws anything attached to a player even in first person (it skips only the camera player itself,
  `LevelExtractor.extractVisibleEntities`) and never tells the server which view it is in, so a server cannot hide them
  in first person only. `/trinketview` (anyone, no op) toggles your own copies on or off, remembered in
  `config/foundry/worn_self_view.json`; with it on they show in first person too. Your own copies are separate displays
  sent only to you, drawn ahead by your ping plus a tick (at most six ticks, at most 45 degrees), because your own model
  turns at once on your screen and anything from the server arrives a round trip later. The copies everyone else sees
  are smoothed over three ticks, the same as their clients smooth your head and body, so they stay together. The circlet
  still goes to others as a helmet. The gills sit beside the neck, behind the eyes.
- **Antler circlet.** Just the antlers (no band, gem or leaves), 10% bigger, rising from the top of the head at the
  temples. They are served on the head to everyone, the wearer included, as a head item the client draws on the head
  itself (exact, and hidden in first person like any helmet): the antlers alone with no helmet on, or with a vanilla
  helmet on, a copy of that helmet with its equipment asset removed and its model set to "antlers on that helmet"
  (`kingdoms:antler_circlet_on_<material>`: the vanilla helmet shell from the vanilla armour texture with the antlers on
  top; leather keeps its dye, enchanted helmets keep their glint, trims do not show). The real helmet is never touched.
  The vanilla armour textures reach the items atlas through `assets/minecraft/atlases/items.json` (Polymer merges atlas
  files), so nothing of Mojang's is copied. Under a head item that is not a vanilla helmet (a pumpkin, a skull) the
  antlers fall back to a display. The wearer's copy sits in the helmet slot of their own inventory screen. Every served
  copy is marked, and `CreativeSlotGuard` (a server mixin) refuses a marked item sent back by a creative client, which is
  how a second pair appeared before; the Bedrock stand in elytra is marked too. Not for Bedrock wearers.
- **Riding displays below the feet (fixed).** A riding display's transformation starts at the top of the player, where
  the client seats it. Until a viewer's client has seated it (just after it appears, a respawn, a teleport) it stood at
  the feet, a body height too low; its own position is now the top of the player too.
- **Facing (fixed 2026-11-01).** A model's front shows toward the display's -z. Body items (cape, wings, pauldron) get a
  half turn so they face out from the back; before the fix the wings were mirrored and the cape showed its lining.
- **3D models.** Each trinket has a real 3D model, not a flat sprite: `examples/assets/kingdoms/models/item/worn/*.json`
  (cuboids with per face UVs, rotations and display transforms) with `examples/assets/kingdoms/textures/item/worn/*.png`,
  made by `foundry/tools/gen_trinket_models.py` (change a number, re-run). `foundry/tools/check_models.py` validates them
  (every face has a texture, UVs in range, legal rotations, modest cuboid count) and `--preview out.png` draws front,
  side and top silhouettes. An item names its model with `"worn_model"`; Foundry then writes a client item definition
  that shows the 3D model in every display context (worn display, helmet slot, inventory, hand, item frame) and keeps
  the flat texture as the fallback and as the Bedrock icon. Only a live client shows the real look and whether a
  rotation direction needs flipping (the cape flare, the antler lean).
- **If the 3D models do not show.** `/foundry worn list` now also prints, per display, the stack the client is sent
  (base item, `item_model`, `custom_model_data`) and the display context. A gametest asserts the client gets
  `item_model=kingdoms:<id>` for all five items (for example `minecraft:iron_ingot` + `kingdoms:forgeman_pauldron`), and
  another that the pack's select covers every display context the game has (all but `none`). So the server side is
  proven; if a player still sees the flat sprite, their client is not using the current pack. Check: hold the item and
  open the inventory (the icon is 3D when the pack is loaded, flat when not), reconnect and accept the server pack,
  Options, Resource Packs, and look for a stale cached copy. Polymer rebuilds the pack on `/foundry pack build` or
  `/polymer generate-pack`; clients fetch it on rejoin.
- **Pink and black (fixed 2026-11-01).** The client only stitches model textures from `textures/item/` (the items
  atlas) and `textures/block/`; the worn textures used to sit in `textures/worn/`, so every face drew the missing texture.
  They now live in `textures/item/worn/` and the models name `kingdoms:item/worn/<id>`. Foundry's validator, `check_models.py`
  and the preview renderer all treat a model texture outside those folders as an error (the preview draws it pink and
  black, as the game does). On the server, `config/foundry/assets/kingdoms/textures/worn/` is no longer used.
- **Following the player (rebuilt again 2026-11-01).** The server's own body yaw is wrong for this: just before each
  player tick it copies the position into the "last position" (`ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.tickPlayer`), so it never
  sees the player walk and its body only trails the head, while every client turns the body toward the walking
  direction. So `foundry.core.BodyYaw` repeats the client's rule (`LivingEntity.tick` and `tickHeadTurn`: aim at the walk
  direction, turned round when walking backwards, ease 30% a tick, never more than 50 degrees from the head, 15 while
  blocking) from the positions Foundry sees each tick, and keeps the cape's trailing point as `Player.moveCloak` does.
  Each display's whole transformation is then built every tick in the holder's own `onTick` (as Polymer's entity models
  do, so it goes out in the same tick) by `WornDisplays.transform`, the way vanilla draws a player: body items turn with
  that body yaw, head items pivot at the neck and follow the head's yaw and pitch, crouching lowers and leans, gliding
  and swimming tip the body forward, invisible or sleeping players show nothing. Interpolation is one tick; riding
  displays are moved by the client every frame. Core checks cover the body rule (walking sideways, backwards, turning in
  place) and the cape swing; gametests cover head against body, pitch, crouching, the cape hang and invisibility. The
  approach follows Patbox's Polymer model handlers (LGPL-3.0), no code copied. Unverified live: how it looks in motion.
- **Textures and previews.** Every face of every cuboid now has its own painted tile at 4 texels per model unit, made

  five.

  never clash). They are race locked and never wear out. Java: Trinkets Updated already lets a glider in a trinket slot

## Maps for Xaero's

Kingdoms and their borders show in colour on Xaero's World Map and Minimap for Java players who install the Charter

- **What players install.** Java Edition only: Fabric, Fabric API, Xaero's World Map 1.47.0 or newer and/or Xaero's
  Minimap 26.6.0 or newer (Modrinth `xaeros-world-map`, `xaeros-minimap`, both for 26.3), and the Charter Maps jar.
  Either Xaero mod alone works. Bedrock has no Xaero's maps; nothing changes for Bedrock players.
- **Handing out the jar.** It is not on Modrinth. Share `MrPinoys_CharterMaps-0.1.0.jar` from `dist/` the way the

- **Who is sent the map.** Only a player whose client registered the `charter:map` channel (Fabric's `canSend`), so
  only clients with the mod. Vanilla Java clients never register it and Floodgate players (UUID high half zero) are
  skipped even if the proxy ever did. The channel is never required: nobody is kicked, warned or logged for not having
  the mod. Polymer and Geyser never see the payload because it is never sent to those players.

  changes: land events (claims, keeps placed, moved or lost, outposts, unclaims) on the next tick, markers at once,
  and a rebuild every five seconds that catches what moves no land event (a Nexus set down, a rename, a race label or a

## Ranks and tags

Every kingdom has a ladder of ranks (titles), top first: the top rank always belongs to whoever holds the decision
office (the Lord, which a kingdom renames King or Queen), and the last rank is the default every other member holds.
A kingdom starts with two, "Lord" and "Commoner", and nothing is stored until a Lord edits; an old save loads exactly
so, every member a Commoner and the Lord at the top. A kingdom has at most 12 ranks. The top rank can only be renamed;
the default rank can be renamed but not removed or moved; the others can be added, renamed, moved up or down and
removed (their holders go back to the default rank). Names are 1 to 20 characters, spaces at the ends cut, with no
colour codes, brackets, braces, bars, backslashes, control or formatting characters. A member holds exactly one rank;
leaving the kingdom forgets it. Model: `charter.org.Ranks`; actions `rank.add`, `rank.rename`, `rank.remove`,
`rank.move`, `rank.give`, `rank.set_power`, each with a Chronicle line (moving is not told).

Who may: the Lord, an operator, the kingdom by its own vote, or anyone given the new power `MANAGE_RANKS` (in the
"Members and offices" group of Give a power). Lords of kingdoms founded before ranks are given `MANAGE_RANKS` on load
(repair note `lord_power_added`). Only the Lord (or an operator) sets what a rank may do, and only the Lord gives or
takes a rank that carries powers.

Privileges: a rank's powers are ordinary grants to the rank, derived from the Lord's own passable grant, so a rank
never carries more than the Lord could pass on and loses a power the moment the Lord does. A member's powers are their
own grants plus their rank's, worked out live by `AuthorityResolver`. Amending the charter, dissolving, appointing to
offices, making sub-factions and managing ranks are never given to a rank. Default ranks carry nothing, so nothing
changes until a Lord opts in.

Where: the Leader's Desk has a Ranks page (rename with a dropdown of King, Queen, Prince, Princess, Duke, Duchess,
Knight, Royal Commander, Commoner, Peasant or a typed name; move up and down; add; remove with a confirm; Give a rank
with a member dropdown and a rank dropdown; What it may do, grouped as Give a power). Where I stand shows your rank;
My faction has a read only Ranks list with who holds each. Commands, behind the same checks:
`/charter rank [list]`, `/charter rank rename <rank> <name>`, `/charter rank add <name>`, `/charter rank remove <rank>`,
`/charter rank up|down <rank>`, `/charter rank set <player> <rank>`, `/charter rank power <rank> <power> on|off`, and
for operators the same under `/charter admin faction title <kingdom> ...`.

Tags: once a kingdom's ranks were edited, a member's tag reads `[Faeloria] Queen Heather`: the bracket in the kingdom
colour, the rank in grey. That takes one scoreboard team per kingdom and rank (`ct_<kingdom part><hash>.<rank>`, at most
12 a kingdom); empty `ct_` teams are dropped on each refresh. A kingdom whose ranks were never edited keeps its one
`ct_<kingdom>` team and the old tag. Mercenaries keep `[Mercenary]` with no rank. Knobs in `charter.json`:
`showRankTags` (default true) shows the rank in the tag; `rankInChat` (default true) off shows only the kingdom tag in
chat while the head and tab list keep the rank.

Bedrock (through Geyser), expected but unconfirmed until a Bedrock client looks: Geyser builds the Bedrock name tag from
the Java team prefix, so the head should read the same, with the kingdom colour rounded to the nearest of Bedrock's 16

## Moving a keep

`/charter admin faction move "<kingdom>" <x> <y> <z>` moves the kingdom's keep so its core chunk is the one at those
coordinates (`MoveKeep`). The keep keeps its identity: members, race, treasury, history, outposts and the leader stay.
Its 9 by 9 plot is laid down again around the new chunk, the old Nexus lodestone is removed and a new one is placed at
the coordinates, lifted clear of ground. The same checks as founding apply: the new plot must not overlap other land and
must keep the gap to other keeps (the keep's own old land does not count). Outposts that no longer touch the keep fall
back on the usual grace for land without a core. The Chronicle gets one line, "moved its keep from A to B". Walls built
by hand around the old plot are not moved. Run it while standing near the new spot so the chunk is loaded.

## Moving the lodestone

`/charter nexus move` moves a kingdom's lodestone to where its Lord stands, anywhere inside the keep's nine chunks. The keep, its land and its core chunk (vault, granary, lectern) stay where they are; only the lodestone block and its record move, and the keep teleport and the jail follow it. The Lord, anyone with the place-core power, and operators may do it. It is refused outside the keep, for a player with no kingdom, and while the keep is open to attack. The keep lectern does not follow the lodestone; it moves on its own (see "Moving the keep lectern" under Terminals).

The natural way needs no typing: the Lord mines the lodestone and places it somewhere else. Code: `charter/fabric/src/main/java/charter/mc/land/NexusLift.java` (and `LiftBook.java` for the pure part).

- **Who may lift it.** The Lord, anyone with the place-core power in that kingdom, and operators. Anyone else who mines it gets "The nexus cannot be broken while its keep stands." as before, and nothing happens. NPC keeps and, for non operators, a Nexus inside the spawn cannot be lifted.
- **What they carry.** Mining it never drops a plain lodestone. It becomes a bound item: a glinting lodestone named "Lifted Nexus of <kingdom>", stacking to one, with custom data naming the lift, the keep and the carrier. It cannot be dropped (it goes back into the inventory), put in a chest, hopper, bundle, item frame or armor stand, or placed by anyone else; a copy in anyone else's inventory is removed within a second.
- **Setting it down.** Place it anywhere inside that kingdom's own keep chunks: the lodestone stands there and its record moves, exactly as `/charter nexus move` does. The keep, its land, vault, granary and the keep lectern stay put. Placing it anywhere else is refused and the item stays in hand. An operator's lifted Nexus is not held to the claim rules, but it still goes only inside its own keep.
- **While it is up.** `/keep` (and `/hub` for the admin kingdom) arrives at the top of the middle of the keep's core chunk with the line "Your lodestone is being moved, so you arrived at the middle of the keep." `/charter nexus move` is refused until it is set down. The Nexus does not open the menu while it is carried; the keep lectern still does.
- **It goes back where it stood** when the carrier dies, logs out (after `nexusLiftLogoutSeconds`, default 0: at once), carries it longer than `nexusLiftMinutes` (default 10), or loses the item some other way, and within a second when the kingdom enters a war or the keep stops being protected. If something was built where it stood, it goes on top. A lift is saved in the world's `charter/nexus_lifts.json` on every change, so a crash or a stop mid lift puts the lodestone back on the next start; the stale item is swept when its carrier joins.
- **No escape from war.** It cannot be lifted while the kingdom is named in any war goal (attacker or defender) or the keep is not protected (vulnerable, under siege). An operator's `/charter admin faction move` cancels a lift: the moved keep gets its new lodestone anyway.
- Only a live server can confirm how mining feels in survival (the break animation runs to the end, then the lodestone vanishes into the inventory) and that Bedrock players see the bound item's name and glint through Geyser.

## The hub

The admin kingdom is the server's spawn town. `/hub` (also `/charter hub`) takes anyone to its Nexus, whatever kingdom they belong to, after the usual teleport wait and cooldown. Its keep is never closed to visitors, even with `closedKeeps` on. With the spawn rules on (the default) only operators build there, the admin kingdom's own members included; see "Spawn rules" below. With more than one admin kingdom the first one is the hub.

## Keep pads in spawn

A keep pad is a block in the spawn town that takes a player to their own keep when they walk onto it, so Bedrock and console players, who cannot type `/keep`, can travel home. Code: `charter/fabric/src/main/java/charter/mc/pad/`.

- **Making one (operator, level 2).** Build the pad block (any block), stand on it and type `/charter admin pad add keep`. The block under your feet becomes the pad; a block that is already a pad is refused. `/charter admin pad remove` removes the pad you stand on, or else the one you look at (within 8 blocks). `/charter admin pad list` shows every pad, each a link that teleports you there. `/charter admin pad clear` removes them all. Only `keep` pads exist today; home, outpost, hub and spawn pads are a one line change (`PadBook.KINDS`).
- **What players see.** A ring of end rod sparks with a few portal motes over each pad every 1.5 seconds, a soft chime when they step on, and the ender teleport sound when they arrive. Then exactly what `/keep` does: the same checks and messages, the "Hold still" wait (the action bar adds "Hold still on the pad."; moving or being hurt cancels it), the cooldown, and arrival at the Nexus. A player with no kingdom is told "You belong to no organization." and to open their Charter book. With `requireAffiliation` on such a player can still walk, so the pad still answers them.
- **When it fires.** Once per visit, when the player is on the pad and has stopped moving; walking straight across does nothing. They step off and back on to try again (jumping in place does not count). If the wait is cancelled while they are still on the pad, it starts again once they stand still. A player who logs in standing on a pad is not sent anywhere until they step off and back on. Riders trigger pads by where their mount stands, with no need to dismount; the rider arrives alone and the mount stays behind on the pad.
- **Knobs** in `charter.json`: `padParticles` (true) and `padSounds` (true). The wait and cooldown are the usual `teleportWarmupSeconds` and `teleportCooldownSeconds`.
- **Saved** in the world's `charter/pads.json`, written on every change and with the world. A pad is only a position: if its block is broken or changed to air the pad stays registered (and still works for anyone standing there); remove it with the command.
- **Do not** make the block `/hub` lands on (the top of the hub's Nexus lodestone) a pad: every `/hub` arrival would fire it at once and be told to wait out the cooldown.
- Unverified on a live server: whether the end rod and portal particles show on Bedrock through Geyser (end rod already does for `keepBorderGlow`), whether Floodgate players' feet are seen on the pad the same way, and the look of the cue. No floating label is shown: Charter has no Polymer dependency, and display entities break Geyser.

## Terminals

A terminal is a block that opens the Charter menu when right-clicked: the player's own Home (Member Home, or Newcomer Home for a player with no kingdom), as a native form on Bedrock and a dialog on Java, exactly what the book or `/charter` opens. It is how console and Bedrock players reach the menu without carrying the book. Code: `charter/fabric/src/main/java/charter/mc/terminal/`.

- **Where they are.** Every keep gets a lectern terminal beside its Nexus, inside the core chunk, when the keep is founded or moved (`keepTerminals`, on by default); a moved keep's old lectern is taken away, a lost keep's lectern stays as a plain block and stops being a terminal. The Nexus lodestone itself also opens the menu for that kingdom's own members (not for anyone else; a compass in hand is left to vanilla so it still points at the lodestone). Operators put as many as they like in spawn.
- **Making them (operator, level 2).** `/charter admin terminal here` places a lectern at your feet, facing you, and registers it. `/charter admin terminal add` makes the block you look at (within 8 blocks) a terminal, any block at all. `/charter admin terminal remove` removes the terminal you look at, or else the one under you; the block stays. `/charter admin terminal list` shows every terminal, each a link that teleports you there (keep terminals are marked). `/charter admin terminal clear` removes them all; the blocks stay. `/charter admin terminal keeps` puts a lectern terminal beside the Nexus of every keep that has no keep terminal yet (keeps founded before this build); NPC keeps and a lifted lodestone are skipped, and it says how many keeps had no free side.
- **To set up spawn:** stand where each terminal should be (by the arrival point, the pads, the market) and type `/charter admin terminal here`; step back and right-click it to check; `/charter admin terminal list` to see them all. Keep terminals appear by themselves for new keeps; after deploying this build, type `/charter admin terminal keeps` once so every existing keep gets its lectern. For a keep it reports as having no free side, stand beside its Nexus and type `/charter admin terminal here` (that terminal is not tied to the keep, so a later keep move leaves it in place).
- **Who can use one.** Everyone, everywhere: the click is answered ahead of every protection rule, so it works for a player with no kingdom and no race under `requireAffiliation`, inside the spawn where a plain lectern is refused, and in another kingdom's land. A held button opens the menu once. Sneaking with something in hand places against the terminal as vanilla does, so build against a terminal (or your own Nexus) while sneaking; a plain right-click opens the menu instead.
- **What players see.** A few end rod sparks over each terminal every two seconds within 24 blocks (`terminalCues`, on by default). End rod is the particle the pads and border glow use because Geyser maps it to Bedrock. No floating label: display entities break Geyser.
- **Saved** in the world's `charter/terminals.json` (written to a temporary file, then moved over the old one), on every change and with the world. A terminal is only a position: break its block and the terminal stays registered; remove it with the command. A keep's own lectern is the exception: it cannot be broken (see below). A terminal lectern never gets a Chronicle book.
- Unverified on a live server: that the sparks show on Bedrock, and that a Bedrock right-click on a lectern reaches the server as a use on that block for every console.

### Moving the keep lectern

A keep's lectern can move anywhere inside the keep's **core chunk** (the chunk with the vault and the granary), never the other eight. The keep, its Nexus and its land stay where they are. Code: `charter/fabric/src/main/java/charter/mc/land/LecternLift.java` and `LecternMove.java`.

- **By hand.** The Lord, anyone with the place-core power in that kingdom, and operators mine the keep lectern and carry it as a bound item: a glinting lectern named "Lifted lectern of <kingdom>", stacking to one. It follows the lifted Nexus rules: it cannot be dropped, stored, framed or placed by anyone else, and a copy in anyone else's inventory is removed. Place it anywhere in the core chunk and it stands there (facing you) as the keep's terminal again; anywhere else is refused and the item stays. While it is carried the old spot neither sparks nor opens the menu, and holding it never opens the menu either, so a click with it in hand sets it down.
- **Nobody else breaks it.** Anyone else who mines the keep lectern is told it stays put and nothing happens. This is new: before, a member who could build in the keep could break it like any block.
- **It goes back where it stood** with the same knobs as the lodestone: when the carrier dies, logs out (after `nexusLiftLogoutSeconds`), carries it longer than `nexusLiftMinutes`, or loses it, and within a second when the kingdom enters a war or the keep stops being protected. It cannot be lifted at war either. Put back, it faces away from the Nexus. A lift is saved in the world's `charter/lectern_lifts.json`, so a stop mid lift puts it back on the next start. The lodestone and the lectern are separate lifts: one player can carry each.
- **By command.** `/charter lectern move` (the Lord, the place-core power, operators) moves the keep lectern to where you stand, facing you. `/charter admin faction lectern "<kingdom>" [<x> <y> <z>]` (operators) moves another kingdom's to your feet, or to the coordinates; from the console give coordinates. Both are refused outside the core chunk, while the kingdom is at war or the keep is open to attack, for NPC kingdoms (operator form), and for a keep that has no keep lectern (`/charter admin terminal keeps` gives it one). `/charter lectern move` is refused while the lectern is carried; the operator form ends a lift under way once every check has passed, and the carrier loses the item.
- **Chronicle.** An operator's move adds "On <date>, the keep lectern of <kingdom> was moved by an operator." A Lord's move, like a Lord's Nexus move, is not written down.
- **A moved keep.** `/charter admin faction move` still puts a new lectern beside the new Nexus and takes the old one; a lectern being carried is not put back, and the carrier loses the item. `/charter admin terminal keeps` skips a keep whose lectern is being carried.
- `/keep`, `/hub` and the book point at the Nexus, not the lectern, so nothing else changes when it moves.
- Only a live server can confirm how mining the lectern feels in survival and that Bedrock players see the item's name and glint.

## The Charter book

The book item is named just "Charter" (2026-11-03; it was the Charter of Rights and Freedoms). Nobody is handed it any more: not on joining, not on respawn and not when one is lost (2026-11-02). Terminals (the keep lecterns) open the same menu, for players with no kingdom and inside the spawn too, and a player who wants a book to carry takes one from there. Code: `charter/fabric/src/main/java/charter/mc/book/` (`BookMod`, `BookRules`, `BoundItems`, `CharterBook`).

- **One copy, your own.** Each book carries its owner's id. A player carries one copy: extra copies, and any copy that belongs to someone else, are removed at login, on respawn and once a second. A book made before books had owners is adopted by whoever carries it.
- **It stays with you.** It never drops on death: it is set aside as the player dies and handed back on respawn (a new one is given only if they then carry none). A dropped book can be picked up only by its owner and is gone after twenty seconds. Chests, barrels, shulker boxes, hoppers, bundles, item frames, armor stands and allays refuse it.
- **"Get a new Charter book".** A button on the Newcomer Home (under More when the Home is full) and under More on a Member Home, so a terminal gives one. It is the only way to a new copy. It gives a copy if the player carries none; if they already carry one it says "You already carry your Charter book. You can only carry one." and gives nothing.
- The `bookGivenTo` and `replaceLostBook` knobs are gone. An old `charter.json` that still has them loads as before; the two keys are ignored.

## The lang override trap

The live server has an old `config/charter/en_us.json`, copied out of the jar on first boot, that overrides the lang text in the jar (`Text.load`). Every key in that file wins over the jar, so a key whose wording a later jar changed keeps showing the old wording, and a key a later jar renamed or reworded for new placeholders can read wrong, until the override is regenerated or deleted. Keys that are not in the file at all fall back to the jar's text, so brand new keys do show; but treat the whole file as stale and delete it (the next start writes a fresh copy) or regenerate it whenever a deploy changes wording. The terminals, book and lodestone work changed no existing key and added these, none of which the old override has: `terminal.look_at`, `terminal.taken`, `terminal.added`, `terminal.placed`, `terminal.here_blocked`, `terminal.none_here`, `terminal.removed`, `terminal.list_none`, `terminal.list_head`, `terminal.list_line`, `terminal.list_line_keep`, `terminal.cleared`, `terminal.keeps_done`, `terminal.keeps_off`, `book.button.new_book`, `book.new.given`, `book.new.already`, `book.not_yours`, `bound.refused`, `nexus_lift.lifted`, `nexus_lift.lifted_short`, `nexus_lift.at_war`, `nexus_lift.one_at_a_time`, `nexus_lift.no_room`, `nexus_lift.item.name`, `nexus_lift.item.lore`, `nexus_lift.item.lore2`, `nexus_lift.stale`, `nexus_lift.outside`, `nexus_lift.outside_hint`, `nexus_lift.placed`, `nexus_lift.back_death`, `nexus_lift.back_exposed`, `nexus_lift.back_logout`, `nexus_lift.back_lost`, `nexus_lift.back_timeout`, `nexus_lift.back_short`, `nexus_lift.busy`, `nexus_lift.arrive_middle`. If an operator ever adds any of them to the override, that text wins from then on.

## Join or sign on before you act

With `requireAffiliation` on (the default), a player who belongs to no kingdom and has no race can only look around: they cannot break, place, use or attack anything, in the wild as well, and eating or any other item use is refused. The Charter book still opens, which is how they join a kingdom or sign on as a mercenary (`/charter merc <race>`). A mercenary or a member acts as normal, and operators are exempt. The knob lives in `charter.json`.

## Spawn rules

Every claim an admin kingdom holds (its keep's core and ring chunks, and any other claim it has) behaves like the spawn of a public server: players may walk, trade, ride, open doors and press buttons; they cannot fight, build, grief or be hurt. The rules apply only to players who are not operators, and (with `adminKingdomBypass` on) not to the admin kingdom's own members; see "The admin kingdom pass". Operators are exempt from all of them, so they can build and fix things. Creative mode alone is no exemption. The code is `charter.mc.land.SpawnRules` (with the `Spawn*` mixins), the pure decisions are `charter.config.SpawnPolicy`, and the tests are `SpawnRulesGameTest` and `SpawnPolicyTest`.

The rules live in `config/charter/charter.json`. `spawnRulesEnabled` (default `true`) switches the whole set; with it off, an admin kingdom's land is protected like any other kingdom's (members build, outsiders walk). Each rule sits in the `spawnRules` section. A key left out keeps its default, so to switch one rule off write only that key, for example:

```json
"spawnRules": { "spawnInvulnerable": false, "spawnAllowedUse": ["#minecraft:doors", "minecraft:lever"] }
```

Restart the server for a change to take effect. After `/charter admin faction admin <kingdom> true|false` the spawn's land is recomputed at once.

Players:

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `spawnNoPvp` | true | No player hurts another (melee, arrows, tridents, splash potions, fire aspect, TNT or crystals they set off) when either of them stands inside. |
| `spawnInvulnerable` | true | Players inside take no damage at all: falls, fire, lava, drowning, suffocation, cactus, wither, starving. The void and `/kill` are not covered. |
| `spawnNoFallDamage` | true | No fall damage inside, also when `spawnInvulnerable` is off. |
| `spawnNoHunger` | true | Food is topped up once a second while a player stands inside. |
| `spawnKeepInventory` | true | A player who dies inside drops nothing and keeps items and experience on respawn. |
| `spawnVoidRescue` | true | A player falling out of the world under the spawn is put back on its Nexus instead of dying. |
| `spawnNoHarmfulEffects` | true | Harmful effects from someone else (splash and lingering potions, tipped arrows) do not land on players inside. |
| `spawnNoPearlsIn` | true | An ender pearl or chorus fruit cannot carry a player into the spawn from outside. Thrown or eaten inside, they work. |
| `spawnNoProjectiles` | true | Bows, crossbows, tridents, snowballs, eggs, fishing rods, wind charges and splash or lingering potions cannot be used inside. |
| `spawnAllowElytraBoost` | true | Fireworks may boost an elytra flight inside. |
| `spawnAllowRiptide` | true | A Riptide trident may launch its holder inside. |
| `spawnAllowItemDrop` | true | Players may drop items inside. Off: a dropped stack goes back into the inventory. |

Mobs:

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `spawnProtectMobs` | true | Players cannot hurt any mob inside (animals, villagers, golems, pets, named and leashed mobs, hostile ones), nor hit a mob outside while standing inside. |
| `spawnNoHostileSpawns` | true | No hostile mob is added inside: natural spawns, spawners, patrols, raids, phantoms. An operator's `/summon` of a hostile mob inside is refused too. |
| `spawnRemoveHostiles` | true | Hostile mobs inside, near any player, are removed once a second. Named ones are left alone. |
| `spawnNoMobGriefing` | true | Sheep do not eat grass, rabbits do not raid carrots, farmer villagers do not harvest or replant. |
| `spawnAllowTrading` | true | Players may trade with villagers and wandering traders (not with a name tag, lead or spawn egg in hand). |
| `spawnAllowRiding` | true | Players may ride boats, plain minecarts and horses with an empty hand, so nothing is fed, saddled or opened. |

Blocks and the world:

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `spawnNoBuild` | true | No breaking, placing, buckets, flint and steel, bone meal, frames, armor stands, spawn eggs, signs or containers, for the admin kingdom's own members too. Entities (frames, stands, carts, animals) cannot be hit or used. |
| `spawnAllowedUse` | doors, trapdoors, fence gates, buttons, lever, bell, crafting table, enchanting table, ender chest, stonecutter, smithing table, loom, cartography table, grindstone | Blocks anyone may right-click inside, by id (`minecraft:lever`) or tag (`#minecraft:doors`). A waxed sign may always be read and its click commands run. Use is let through only with an empty hand or when not sneaking, and an item's own use on the block is still refused. |
| `spawnNoExplosions` | true | No explosion breaks blocks inside, or hurts or pushes anything standing there, unless an operator set it off. |
| `spawnNoFire` | true | No fire spreads, burns blocks or is lit by lava inside. |
| `spawnSealBorder` | true | Water and lava, pistons (the piston head too), dispensers and hoppers do not cross the spawn's border in either direction. Inside, an operator's fountain still flows. |
| `spawnFreezeBlocks` | true | Random ticks do nothing inside: no crop, sapling, grass or vine growth, no leaf decay, no ice or snow melting, no farmland drying, no copper ageing. |
| `spawnNoSnowAndIce` | true | The weather piles no snow and freezes no water inside. |
| `spawnNoLightning` | true | A lightning bolt inside goes out before it lights fires, charges creepers or turns villagers into witches. |
| `spawnNoPortalCreation` | true | Arriving through a nether portal never builds a new portal frame inside, or anywhere the game could place it within 16 blocks of the spawn. An existing portal there is still used; with none, the trip from the Nether does not happen. |
| `spawnNoProjectileBlockHits` | true | A projectile (not an operator's) hitting a block inside leaves it as it is: no TNT lit by flame arrows, no broken pots, no chorus or target reactions. |
| `spawnEntryNotice` | true | Entering the spawn reads "You are in the spawn area. PvP, building and mob fighting are off here." instead of the kingdom line. |

Players with no kingdom and no race (`requireAffiliation`) may still walk the spawn, open its allowlisted doors and buttons and open their Charter book; everything else stays refused for them, trading included.

Left out on purpose: lecterns are not on the allowlist (a lectern menu lets anyone take the book, and the Charter book is not a book a lectern holds); anvils are not either (they wear out and break with use); chests, barrels and other containers are refused. A fishing rod cast from outside can still hook a player inside; falling blocks and frost walker are not stopped (only operators build there); turtle eggs and coral are not guarded.

## No friendly fire in a kingdom

`kingdomFriendlyFire` (top level of `charter.json`, default `false`). Off, a member of a kingdom cannot hurt another member of the same kingdom: not by hand, not with arrows, tridents or other projectiles, not with lit TNT they primed and not through a tamed wolf. The hit simply does nothing. Kingdoms are compared at the top, so outposts and offices of one kingdom count as one people. Players from different kingdoms, mercenaries and players with no kingdom fight as before, and a player can still hurt themselves. On, members can fight each other like anyone else.

## Carried mounts (the carry mod rising bug)

`carryGuard` (top level of `charter.json`, default `true`). The server runs `serverside-carryon`, a third party mod with no config. It carries a mob or a player by switching off its AI, gravity and physics and moving it to a hold position beside the carrier every tick. If someone mounts a carried horse, the horse is moved relative to its rider each tick, and the pair climbs into the sky without end.

Vanilla mounts never have their physics switched off, so once a tick Charter sets down any player whose mount (or anything below it) has no physics, and a carried player throws off anyone riding on them. The player is told: "You were set down. Nobody can ride something that is being carried; wait until it is put down." Spectators have no physics by design and are left alone. There is no clean hook to refuse the mount itself, so the rider may be on the carried horse for one tick before being set down. Off: Charter does not interfere.

## The locator bar for admins only

`locatorBarAdminsOnly` (top level of `charter.json`, default `true`). The vanilla locator bar (the player markers on the
experience bar) is switched by the world wide `locator_bar` game rule. With the rule on and this knob on, only players
holding `charter.locatorbar` see anyone on the bar. LuckPerms gives it to the admin group
(`deploy/server-hardening/luckperms-groups.txt`); with no permissions mod, operators have it. Everyone else gets a
transient modifier that takes their `minecraft:waypoint_receive_range` to 0, so their bar stays empty, while their
transmit range is untouched and they still show on an admin's bar. The modifier is never saved with the player. It is
set on join, respawn and dimension change and refreshed every 3 seconds, so granting or removing the node takes effect
without a relog. Off, the modifier is removed from everyone and the game rule alone decides. With the game rule off
nobody sees the bar whatever this knob says.

Bedrock is unverified: it is not known whether Geyser shows Bedrock players a locator bar at all. If it does, the same
receive range applies to them, since the server decides who is sent.

Test: `LocatorBarGameTest`.

## Explosions in kingdoms

`explosionsInKingdoms` (top level of `charter.json`, default `false`). Off, no explosion breaks or sets fire to a block on any claimed land: keeps (core and ring), outposts whether raised or still raising, every other claim, and the admin kingdom, whether or not the claim is protected at the time and whoever lit it, the owner and operators included. That covers TNT, TNT minecarts, creepers, ghast fireballs, withers and wither skulls, end crystals, beds in the Nether, respawn anchors, wind charges and any modded source, because all of them pass through the one list of blocks an explosion is about to break. The fire an explosion starts and the doors and buttons a wind charge toggles use the same list, so they are covered too. The wild is unchanged. Damage to players and mobs is unchanged, except in the spawn (`spawnNoExplosions`). The Nexus, banners and the mod's other fixtures are always spared.

On, explosions behave as before: protected land is spared unless a permitted player lit the charge, and a claim that is not protected at the moment (a raising outpost, an outpost opened for capture in a war) can be blown up.

Priming TNT on someone else's land is refused by the ordinary claim protection. A member may still place and light TNT on their own land; it just breaks nothing there while the knob is off.
