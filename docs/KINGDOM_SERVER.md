# The kingdom server (d212ecae)

The owner's shipped Fabric server: Charter, Lemon, Foundry, Polymer, Geyser and Floodgate (Bedrock), plus worldgen
packs. Deploy with the `deploy-shipped-server` skill. The player facing handbook is the owner's Server Manual
artifact; this file is for agents.

## Shape of the game

- Five kingdoms, created by ops only (`/charter admin faction create`) and led by a player an op appoints. Each is a
  race: Azrak (Dwarf), Aurelia (Human), Azurius (Merfolk), Faeloria (Fae), Everdelle (Fairy). A kingdom's keep is a
  permanent 9 by 9 chunk square that cannot be lost; the keep granary is off.
- Outposts are the only extra claims (overworld only). They hold points of interest and give fast travel; wars are
  fought between touching outposts. Players cannot found, dissolve or secede (`playerFounding` false).
- Charter publishes a read only map of held land for other mods: `charter/docs/AREAS_API.md`. Structures do not
  generate inside held land (`StructureMixin`).
- World: seed 3806707291501128365, border 10000 across centred on 0,0, the End off. Dungeons and Taverns, Structory
  and Incendium add structures. Pregeneration is partial; the rest generates on demand.

## Race weapons

Five weapons, three tiers each, in `foundry/examples/content`: `kingdoms:azrak_pick_N`, `aurelia_oathblade_N`,
`azurius_trident_N`, `faeloria_staff_N` and `demo:fairy_wand` (tiers 2 and 3 are `_2`, `_3`). All are generated by
`foundry/tools/gen_weapons.py`; never hand edit the JSON, change the script and re-run it. Upgrades are shapeless
recipes with the lower tier first, unlocked through generated advancements.

- Each weapon has `require_permission` `race:<kingdom>`. Charter answers that node for members (`RaceNodes`), through
  the Fabric permission API. Ops pass the lock. A race mod can grant the same nodes later.
- `keep_on_death` keeps the weapon with its owner. `handheld` makes the icon held like a tool.
- Base items are bone (trident), blaze rod (staff), breeze rod (wand), stone pickaxe, iron sword. Never use `stick`:
  Litematica uses it as a client tool.
- Bedrock: Geyser cannot read Polymer models. `foundry/tools/gen_bedrock.py` writes the Geyser mappings and the
  Bedrock pack; deploy with `--bedrock`. Geyser needs `enable-custom-content: true` (already set).

## Races and mercenaries

A player's race is a kingdom's race, stored on `PlayerRecord.race` and read with `CharterState.raceOf`. The weapons ask
`race:<kingdom>` and Charter answers from that race (`RaceNodes`), so a race outlives membership.

- A member gets their kingdom's race when they first join. Leaving does not change it.
- A mercenary is a player with no organization who has chosen a race with the Charter book (Become a mercenary) or
  `/charter merc <kingdom>` (`ChooseMercenaryRace`). They belong to no kingdom, hold no land and are killable anywhere.
  Grouping up is left to the players for now.
- A player cannot choose a race twice. Joining a kingdom that is not mixed makes them of its race (so a mercenary who
  is accepted elsewhere swaps race). Operators can also set it with the same action.
- Admin kingdoms (flag `adminKingdom`, for example Hey Dol Hollow) cannot be chosen as a mercenary race.
- The Mixed Race option (`Organization.mixedRace`, set with `/charter admin faction mixed <kingdom> <on>`) lets a
  kingdom take members who keep their own race. The Freedoms page says which a kingdom is. A leader's own switch for it
  is not built yet. Turning it off again does not change anyone already inside.
- Players see a race's own name, not its kingdom's: `Organization.raceName` (blank falls back to the kingdom name), set
  with `/charter admin faction racename <kingdom> <name>` (Azrak Dwarf, Aurelia Human, Azurius Merfolk, Faeloria Fae,
  Everdelle Fairy).
- Operators set a race with `/charter admin faction race <player> <race>` (any kingdom's race, members too, nothing else about
  the player changes) and remove it with `/charter admin faction race <player> none` (`SetRace`). A player with no race takes
  the race of the next kingdom they join, or chooses as a mercenary. Someone who has a race and no organization can be a
  mercenary of that race.
- Race passives: the `racePassives` knob in `charter.json` maps a race node path to effect ids and `RacePassives`
  refreshes them every 5 seconds for everyone who has the race, members and mercenaries alike. The default gives the
  Merfolk (Azurius) Water Breathing and Dolphin's Grace; the effects fade within seconds of losing the race. Other
  races have none yet; add entries such as `"faeloria": ["minecraft:night_vision"]` to the config to give them some.
- Bedrock has no Dolphin's Grace effect, so `racePassivesBedrock` gives Bedrock players of a race extra effects on top of
  the normal list: by default Azurius gets `water:minecraft:speed#1` (Speed II, only while in water, no icon or
  particles). Entries read `[water:]<effect id>[#amplifier]`. A Floodgate player is recognised by a UUID with a zero high
  half. Untested on a real Bedrock client: whether Bedrock swimming speed responds to Speed is the open question; if it
  does not, change the list in `charter.json` (for example drop it) with no new build.
- Planned, not designed: an altar that swaps a player's race.

## Locks on leaders

Two knobs in `charter.json` (both default true, set both false on the kingdom server): `leadersChangeGovernance` stops
a Lord or council changing the form of government (`SetSeats`), and `leadersAppointOffices` stops players seating anyone
in an office or making a new one (`Appoint`, `CreateOffice`). Operators and the members' elections still work, so a
vacant Lord seat can still be filled by vote or by `/charter admin faction leader`. The "Who decides" page then says the
server sets it and offers no buttons.

## Race looks and traits (replaced the race trinkets, 2026-11-01)

The race trinkets are gone as items to wear. Instead every player always shows their race's look, so anyone can tell
their race at a glance, and the race traits come with the race:

| Race (node) | Look | Trait |
|---|---|---|
| Azrak Dwarves (`race:azrak`) | Forgeman's Pauldron on the right shoulder | Haste (`racePassives`) |
| Aurelia Humans (`race:aurelia`) | Kingdom Cape (the vanilla cape shape) | Luck (`racePassives`) |
| Faeloria Fae (`race:faeloria`) | Antlers, combined with any vanilla helmet | Night Vision (`racePassives`) |
| Azurius Merfolk (`race:azurius`) | Tidal Gills beside the neck | Water Breathing, Dolphin's Grace (`racePassives`) |
| Everdelle Fairies (`race:everdelle`) | Fairy Wings | Glide, always, with nothing in the chest slot |

- The looks are drawn by Foundry from the race node (`races` in `config/foundry/worn_displays.json`: node, item, slot,
  glide). Always on, no toggle; `/trinketview` only decides whether you see your own (the antlers are always on your own
  head, as a helmet item).
- The traits are Charter's `racePassives` (built in defaults, and merged into the server's `charter.json`).
- Fairies glide by race: a server mixin (`LivingEntityGlide`) lets them, holding jump while falling starts it on Java, and
  Bedrock Fairies get the stand in elytra. No durability.
- The five trinket items stay registered as keepsakes ("a retired trinket, kept for something new later") with no slot,
  effect, lock or glider. Trinkets are locked away: anything left in a trinket slot is cleared (logged), and the
  `/charter trinkets` shortcut and book button are gone. Trinkets Updated and its Polymer patch stay installed for now so
  the slots can be repurposed later.
- Everything below about the old trinkets (worn slots, wings in the cape slot) is history; the drawing, following, head
  combining, Bedrock and creative guard notes all still apply to the race looks.

## Race trinkets: opening the screen and how they look

Four race trinkets (Tidal Gills, Forgeman's Pauldron, Kingdom Cape, Antler Circlet) sit in Trinkets Updated slots; the
Fairy Wings are an elytra in the chest slot and show on every client.

- **Opening the Trinkets screen.** Any player, op or not, can use `/trinkets` (the patch registers it with no permission
  check), `/trinkets compact` to switch between the compact and flat layouts, or right click a helmet, chestplate,
  leggings or boots slot in the inventory. `/charter trinkets` runs the same `/trinkets` as the player, and the Charter
  book home has a Trinkets button. Both need no op level. A permission mod that restricts commands would also have to
  allow `trinkets`.
- **What other players see.** A vanilla Java client cannot render Trinkets models (the patch README says trinkets
  "won't render visually"; a resource pack alone cannot draw them). So Foundry draws them itself (`WornDisplays`): one
  Polymer item display per worn Foundry trinket, turned with the body (cape, pauldron) or head (circlet, gills). No
  client mod is needed, only Trinkets on the server. It shows the item's own model (a flat sprite today), appears
  while the trinket is in its slot, and goes on unequip, death, logout, a new player object or a dimension change.
  It is hidden while the player is invisible, swimming, gliding or asleep. Worn trinkets are looked for every 10
  ticks and the per tick turning runs only while someone wears one. The gills are hidden from their own wearer (they
  sit in front of the eyes); the rest show to everyone including the wearer in third person.
- **Tuning it live.** `config/foundry/worn_displays.json` (shipped example: `foundry/examples/config/worn_displays.json`)
  sets `up`, `back`, `side` (blocks from the feet; back is behind, side is to the player's right), `scale`,
  `on_head`, `rotation` [x, y, z] degrees and `hide_from_wearer`, per slot under `slots` and per item under `items`
  (an item entry names its `slot` and inherits it). Any field can be left out. Foundry checks the file every two
  seconds and re-places everyone's displays when it changes, with no restart; a file with a problem logs a warning and
  the last good placements stay. Without the file the built in defaults apply.
  Unverified on a live client: how each item looks and sits, size and facing, and cost with many players. A client with
  the Trinkets mod also gets its own render from `foundry/examples/assets/kingdoms/trinkets/` and may show a second
  copy; delete those files if so.
- **Why the wearer saw nothing at first, and the check command.** Polymer sends an attached display only to players
  tracking the entity, and nobody tracks themselves, so the wearer never got their own displays (a lone player saw
  nothing in F5). `WornDisplays` now adds the wearer by hand, except for a placement with `hide_from_wearer`. Each
  display is also placed before it is attached, so the first packet already has its size and position. `/foundry worn
  list [player]` (op) prints each player's worn Foundry trinkets, the displays made for them, who receives each one and
  where it sits: no display line means "not created" (no placement for that slot, or Trinkets absent); a line with the
  right watchers but nothing on screen means "created but not visible" (try larger `up`, `back`, `side` or `scale`).
  The harness cannot make mock players track each other, so Polymer's real tracking of other players is unverified.
- **No lag.** Two settings in `worn_displays.json`, per slot or item. `head_slot` (default on for `head/hat`, the
  circlet) sends the item to other players as if it were in the helmet slot (a per viewer equipment packet), so it
  follows the head exactly; the real helmet slot is never changed, the wearer is not sent it, and it falls back to a
  display while a real helmet is worn and returns when it comes off. `ride` (default on for the displays) mounts the
  display on the player as a virtual passenger, so the client moves it every frame instead of waiting for position
  packets; heights are then measured from the player's top, which Foundry handles. Set either to false to go back to the
  old behaviour if something looks wrong. Both are unverified on a live client.
- **What the wearer sees (`/trinketview`).** By default you do not see your own trinkets; everyone else does. The client
  draws anything attached to a player even in first person (it skips only the camera player itself,
  `LevelExtractor.extractVisibleEntities`) and never tells the server which view it is in, so a server cannot hide them
  in first person only. `/trinketview` (anyone, no op) toggles your own copies on or off, remembered in
  `config/foundry/worn_self_view.json`; with it on they show in first person too. Your own copies are separate displays
  sent only to you, drawn ahead by your ping plus a tick (at most six ticks, at most 45 degrees), because your own model
  turns at once on your screen and anything from the server arrives a round trip later. The copies everyone else sees
  are smoothed over three ticks, the same as their clients smooth your head and body, so they stay together. The circlet
  still goes to others as a helmet. The gills sit beside the neck, behind the eyes.
- **Antler circlet.** Just the antlers (no band, gem or leaves), 10% bigger, rising from the top of the head at the
  temples. They are served on the head to everyone, the wearer included, as a head item the client draws on the head
  itself (exact, and hidden in first person like any helmet): the antlers alone with no helmet on, or with a vanilla
  helmet on, a copy of that helmet with its equipment asset removed and its model set to "antlers on that helmet"
  (`kingdoms:antler_circlet_on_<material>`: the vanilla helmet shell from the vanilla armour texture with the antlers on
  top; leather keeps its dye, enchanted helmets keep their glint, trims do not show). The real helmet is never touched.
  The vanilla armour textures reach the items atlas through `assets/minecraft/atlases/items.json` (Polymer merges atlas
  files), so nothing of Mojang's is copied. Under a head item that is not a vanilla helmet (a pumpkin, a skull) the
  antlers fall back to a display. The wearer's copy sits in the helmet slot of their own inventory screen. Every served
  copy is marked, and `CreativeSlotGuard` (a server mixin) refuses a marked item sent back by a creative client, which is
  how a second pair appeared before; the Bedrock stand in elytra is marked too. Not for Bedrock wearers.
- **Riding displays below the feet (fixed).** A riding display's transformation starts at the top of the player, where
  the client seats it. Until a viewer's client has seated it (just after it appears, a respawn, a teleport) it stood at
  the feet, a body height too low; its own position is now the top of the player too.
- **Facing (fixed 2026-11-01).** A model's front shows toward the display's -z. Body items (cape, wings, pauldron) get a
  half turn so they face out from the back; before the fix the wings were mirrored and the cape showed its lining.
- **3D models.** Each trinket has a real 3D model, not a flat sprite: `examples/assets/kingdoms/models/item/worn/*.json`
  (cuboids with per face UVs, rotations and display transforms) with `examples/assets/kingdoms/textures/item/worn/*.png`,
  made by `foundry/tools/gen_trinket_models.py` (change a number, re-run). `foundry/tools/check_models.py` validates them
  (every face has a texture, UVs in range, legal rotations, modest cuboid count) and `--preview out.png` draws front,
  side and top silhouettes. An item names its model with `"worn_model"`; Foundry then writes a client item definition
  that shows the 3D model in every display context (worn display, helmet slot, inventory, hand, item frame) and keeps
  the flat texture as the fallback and as the Bedrock icon. Only a live client shows the real look and whether a
  rotation direction needs flipping (the cape flare, the antler lean).
- **If the 3D models do not show.** `/foundry worn list` now also prints, per display, the stack the client is sent
  (base item, `item_model`, `custom_model_data`) and the display context. A gametest asserts the client gets
  `item_model=kingdoms:<id>` for all five items (for example `minecraft:iron_ingot` + `kingdoms:forgeman_pauldron`), and
  another that the pack's select covers every display context the game has (all but `none`). So the server side is
  proven; if a player still sees the flat sprite, their client is not using the current pack. Check: hold the item and
  open the inventory (the icon is 3D when the pack is loaded, flat when not), reconnect and accept the server pack,
  Options, Resource Packs, and look for a stale cached copy. Polymer rebuilds the pack on `/foundry pack build` or
  `/polymer generate-pack`; clients fetch it on rejoin.
- **Pink and black (fixed 2026-11-01).** The client only stitches model textures from `textures/item/` (the items
  atlas) and `textures/block/`; the worn textures used to sit in `textures/worn/`, so every face drew the missing texture.
  They now live in `textures/item/worn/` and the models name `kingdoms:item/worn/<id>`. Foundry's validator, `check_models.py`
  and the preview renderer all treat a model texture outside those folders as an error (the preview draws it pink and
  black, as the game does). On the server, `config/foundry/assets/kingdoms/textures/worn/` is no longer used.
- **Following the player (rebuilt again 2026-11-01).** The server's own body yaw is wrong for this: just before each
  player tick it copies the position into the "last position" (`ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.tickPlayer`), so it never
  sees the player walk and its body only trails the head, while every client turns the body toward the walking
  direction. So `foundry.core.BodyYaw` repeats the client's rule (`LivingEntity.tick` and `tickHeadTurn`: aim at the walk
  direction, turned round when walking backwards, ease 30% a tick, never more than 50 degrees from the head, 15 while
  blocking) from the positions Foundry sees each tick, and keeps the cape's trailing point as `Player.moveCloak` does.
  Each display's whole transformation is then built every tick in the holder's own `onTick` (as Polymer's entity models
  do, so it goes out in the same tick) by `WornDisplays.transform`, the way vanilla draws a player: body items turn with
  that body yaw, head items pivot at the neck and follow the head's yaw and pitch, crouching lowers and leans, gliding
  and swimming tip the body forward, invisible or sleeping players show nothing. Interpolation is one tick; riding
  displays are moved by the client every frame. Core checks cover the body rule (walking sideways, backwards, turning in
  place) and the cape swing; gametests cover head against body, pitch, crouching, the cape hang and invisibility. The
  approach follows Patbox's Polymer model handlers (LGPL-3.0), no code copied. Unverified live: how it looks in motion.
- **Textures and previews.** Every face of every cuboid now has its own painted tile at 4 texels per model unit, made
  by the part's material (brushed iron, bronze, leather with stitching, engraved gold, a faceted gem, ringed antler,
  ridged shell, see through fins) with grain, highlights and bevelled edges. The cape has a gold border and a crowned
  emblem with a darker lining; the wings shade from rose to pale blue with branching veins. `foundry/tools/preview_model.py`
  renders any vanilla item model with its textures from five sides (checked against vanilla's anvil and lantern) and
  draws a texture outside the atlas folders pink and black, as the game would; `tools/previews/contact_sheet.png` shows all
  five.
- **Wings.** The Fairy Wings go in the **cape slot** (`chest/cape`, which has room for two, so the Human cape and the wings
  never clash). They are race locked and never wear out. Java: Trinkets Updated already lets a glider in a trinket slot
  glide on the server; `Wings` starts the glide for a Fairy who holds jump while falling, and stops anyone else.
  Bedrock: the Bedrock client only glides with an elytra in its own chest slot, and Geyser (2.12,
  `SessionPlayerEntity.canStartGliding`) checks the chest item is `minecraft:elytra` with the glider component before it
  forwards "start gliding"; neither knows Trinkets slots, so Bedrock Fairies could not glide. Now a Bedrock player
  wearing allowed wings with an empty chest slot is shown a stand in elytra named "Fairy Wings" in their own chest slot
  (display only: the server never holds it, so taking or dropping it is undone, and it is re-sent every two seconds).
  It goes when the wings come off, a chestplate goes on or the player dies. A Bedrock player in a chestplate cannot glide
  with the wings and is told so once. The four wings are painted already tipped and swing 22.5 degrees back from the
  back in a V; each plate's two faces carry mirror image uvs so the wing outline is right from the front and the back.
  Unverified live: Bedrock gliding (needs a Bedrock Fairy to try), and that a Java client accepts a glide the server
  started.
- **Cape.** The Kingdom Cape is the vanilla cape: the same 10 by 16 by 1 box, hung from the top of the back, and the
  vanilla 64 by 32 cape texture layout, so any cape PNG drawn for vanilla can replace
  `textures/item/worn/kingdom_cape.png`. It swings back the way vanilla's cape layer swings (6 degrees at rest, more as it
  trails a player moving forward or falling, 25 more crouching). It is a display, not the client's own cape: a server
  cannot give a vanilla player a real cape, which comes signed from Mojang's profile servers.
- **Bedrock.** Nothing is drawn, on purpose: the live log showed Geyser throwing `Missing ID 73 in Java registry minecraft:entity_type` for every item display it was sent (it cannot translate `item_display` on 26.3), so `WornDisplays` never sends displays to a Floodgate player (UUID high half zero). Geyser (current master) lists `text_display` as required entity metadata but
  `item_display` and `block_display` as not required, so Polymer's item displays are almost certainly not translated;
  I found no Geyser option to attach a worn model to a player either. The trinket itself still works for Bedrock
  players, and they open the screen with `/trinkets`. Possible routes (none built): a Geyser custom item mapping plus an
  attachable in the Bedrock pack and mirroring the trinket into the real head or chest slot for Bedrock players only
  (medium effort, confusing inventory); an invisible armor stand wearing the item that Geyser does translate (medium,
  but it needs the item mapped and may look wrong); or accepting nothing.

## Coins

`kingdoms:copper_coin`, `kingdoms:iron_coin` and `kingdoms:gold_coin` are plain Foundry items (stack of 64, a drawn 16x16 icon,
nugget look for clients without the pack). They have no recipes, no mechanics and no value yet; they are the items the
kingdom currencies will use. Icons come from `foundry/tools/gen_icons.py` (the `COINS` table). Deploy with
`--content --bedrock`; give one with `/foundry give <player> kingdoms:gold_coin 16`.

## Entering messages and borders

- `Crossings` tells a player on the action bar when they enter a kingdom ("Entering Azrak, land of the Dwarf"), an
  outpost, other held land or the wild. It shows when the place changes and once on joining. The `entryMessages` knob
  turns it off.
- Borders are particle dust (`Signals`): green for your own kingdom, red for others. They show while holding a compass
  or war banner, and `/charter borders` toggles them on without a tool (until the player leaves).
- Xaero maps: a server cannot draw on Xaero's maps by itself. The only route found is Open Parties and Claims (OPAC,
  Fabric 26.3 build exists): Charter would mirror its claims into OPAC through `OpenPACServerAPI` with OPAC's own
  protection off, and players would need OPAC on the client. Not built; whether a server-only install is enough, and
  whether OPAC accepts a made-up owner UUID per kingdom, are unverified. The `areas.json` land map could feed a web map.

## Name tags

`Tags` puts a coloured [tag] before each player's name in chat, the tab list and over their head, using one scoreboard
team per tag (names `ct_<kingdom>`, refreshed every 2 seconds). Members wear their kingdom's name, and a player with a
race and no kingdom wears [Mercenary]. Colours are the `tagColors` knob in `charter.json` (kingdom name in lower case to
a hex colour, plus `mercenary`); a kingdom with no entry gets no tag. Defaults: Everdelle pink, Azurius baby blue,
Faeloria purple, Azrak brown, Aurelia orange, Mercenary blood red. Only teams named `ct_*` are touched, so a player on
another team keeps it. Bedrock players see the tag only if Geyser carries the team prefix, which is unverified.
## Moving a keep

`/charter admin faction move "<kingdom>" <x> <y> <z>` moves the kingdom's keep so its core chunk is the one at those
coordinates (`MoveKeep`). The keep keeps its identity: members, race, treasury, history, outposts and the leader stay.
Its 9 by 9 plot is laid down again around the new chunk, the old Nexus lodestone is removed and a new one is placed at
the coordinates, lifted clear of ground. The same checks as founding apply: the new plot must not overlap other land and
must keep the gap to other keeps (the keep's own old land does not count). Outposts that no longer touch the keep fall
back on the usual grace for land without a core. The Chronicle gets one line, "moved its keep from A to B". Walls built
by hand around the old plot are not moved. Run it while standing near the new spot so the chunk is loaded.

## Closed keeps, border glow and the lost book

All three are server-side, so Java and Bedrock players (through Geyser) see the same thing. Each has a knob in `charter.json`.

- `closedKeeps` (`KeepGate`): a player who is not a member of the keep's kingdom, not at war with it (`WarBook.between`)
  and not an operator is put back where they last stood outside, or just past the nearest edge, a few times a second.
  The action bar says "This keep is closed to strangers." Mercenaries count as strangers. Off by default.
- `keepBorderGlow` (`KeepBorders`): end rod particles along the ground edge of every keep within 40 blocks of a player,
  with nothing in hand. It replaces the hand-built stone walls. End rod maps cleanly to Bedrock; coloured dust may not,
  so the line is one colour. Off by default.
- `replaceLostBook` (on by default): the Charter book is the only way into the menu on console and Bedrock, where there
  is no typing a command, so a player who carries no book is given one within five seconds and on respawn
  (`BookMod.ensure`), not only when they next join.
- Instant villager restock is not in Charter. It is its own small mod, `instantrestock/` (`MrPinoys_InstantRestock`),
  one mixin, no settings.

## Structure salts

`tools/worldgen/kingdom_structure_salts` is a world datapack (installed in `/world/datapacks` on d212ecae) that gives
ancient cities and trial chambers their own placement salt, so a player who learns the world seed still cannot find them
with a finder tool. It affects only chunks generated after it was installed; the world was already about 880 MB of region
files when it went in, so structures in that area stay at their vanilla positions. The seed itself is not hidden: a
client can still work it out from what it sees. Keep the datapack and its salts private. Its README says how to update it.

## Branches and Minecraft versions

Master is on Minecraft 26.3 (Charter, Lemon, Foundry). Pocket Dungeons and the rest are on their own schedule; check
each module's `gradle.properties`. A version bump is its own branch (`feature/minecraft-26-N`): change the loom
versions, then fix what no longer compiles (26.3 moved `Player.drop` to `spawnAtLocation`). Port, build, run the
gametests, then merge to master before deploying so master never lags the server.

## Moving the lodestone

`/charter nexus move` moves a kingdom's lodestone to where its Lord stands, anywhere inside the keep's nine chunks. The keep, its land and its core chunk (vault, granary, lectern) stay where they are; only the lodestone block and its record move, and the keep teleport and the jail follow it. The Lord, anyone with the place-core power, and operators may do it. It is refused outside the keep, for a player with no kingdom, and while the keep is open to attack.

## The hub

The admin kingdom is the server's spawn town. `/hub` (also `/charter hub`) takes anyone to its Nexus, whatever kingdom they belong to, after the usual teleport wait and cooldown. Its keep is never closed to visitors, even with `closedKeeps` on; the land is still protected, so only the kingdom and operators build there. With more than one admin kingdom the first one is the hub.

## Join or sign on before you act

With `requireAffiliation` on (the default), a player who belongs to no kingdom and has no race can only look around: they cannot break, place, use or attack anything, in the wild as well, and eating or any other item use is refused. The Charter book still opens, which is how they join a kingdom or sign on as a mercenary (`/charter merc <race>`). A mercenary or a member acts as normal, and operators are exempt. The knob lives in `charter.json`.
