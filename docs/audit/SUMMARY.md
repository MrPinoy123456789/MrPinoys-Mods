# Audit summary: the kingdom server mod suite (2026-11-04)

Code audited: master at 478cce26. Every test suite passes (Charter 2681 core, 569 fabric, 244 gametests; Currency 416; Fortune 134 and 9; Foundry 597 and 116; Excellent Enchants 18; Instant Restock 3; Lemon 2; Pocket Dungeons 458). The problems below are not test failures. They are things the tests were never pointed at. Coin is in Gold Sovereigns throughout.

## The ten things that matter most, in order

1. **A new player is told to open a book nobody gives them, then stands frozen until a Lord wakes up.** Charter sends no welcome of its own. The first action bar line says "Open your Charter book to choose" (`land.drifter`), but since 2026-11-03 nobody is handed a book; it comes from the lectern, which nothing on screen names. After "Ask to join", `requireAffiliation` blocks breaking, placing and eating until a Lord of that kingdom is online to admit them. Azrak has five people across many timezones. This is the first-30-minutes failure, and it hits Bedrock hardest because the only other cues are chat links. Fix: a join title, reword two lines to point at the sparkling lectern, let applicants act in the wild while they wait. Under a day. (UX-1, GP-1, UX-3, UX-4)

2. **War as built is an offline-capture mechanic that unprotects the target for everyone.** A declaration costs nothing; once open, every claim of the named outpost loses protection to all players, mercenaries included, for 36 siege hours; capture needs ten minutes with no defender in the banner chunk, and progress never decays. The practical play is: wait until the enemy sleeps, stand still. The design document names offline griefing as the thing that kills kingdom servers. The grace period ends 2026-11-06. Fix: scope the protection loss to the attacker, charge a stake, require two or three attackers, decay progress. About two days. (CC-2, CC-3, CC-4)

3. **The economy's scale is ten times off, and the treasury has a back door.** The Mint strikes up to 3,200 Sovereigns a day per player (2,560 kept) from nether gold ore or a bastion, while a keep costs 32, an outpost 4, a fine caps at 8. One player's daily Mint tax buys twenty keeps. Separately, `/treasury withdraw` lets a Lord empty the kingdom's whole treasury with no vote, no limit and no line in Charter's history, bypassing the governance built for exactly that. And Fortune promises "+N Valor" on every spin while the `fortune` earn source is missing from Charter Currency's defaults, so unless an operator added it by hand the Valor never arrives (unverified live). Fix: cut `mint.softCapIngots` to 64, multiply the Charter Marks knobs by ten, make the Currency withdraw honour Charter's limit, ship the `fortune` source. Config plus half a day of code. (EC-1, EC-3, EC-2)

4. **Kingdom land protects blocks, not lives.** Entity protection checks melee and right click only, so an outsider with a bow, trident, splash potion or dispenser can kill a kingdom's horses, villagers, golems and pets and break item frames and armour stands from outside the wall, and nobody can tell who did it. On top of that, the race weapons' area abilities (Ground Slam, Undertow, Quickdraw, both bursts) hit villagers, pets, mounts and allied players of another race inside a keep; Charter's friendly fire rule cannot stop a shove, a root or poison. These will be the first griefs and the first "my weapon is broken" tickets. About two days together. (CF-1, FO-4, CF-10)

5. **One typo in `charter.json` silently unlocks the kingdom.** If any knob fails validation, Charter logs a warning and runs every knob on defaults. The defaults set `leadersChangeGovernance` and `leadersAppointOffices` to true (live: false), reset `racePassives`, `closedKeeps`, `tagColors`. Nothing tells a player or an operator in game. The live `config/charter/en_us.json` override (628 stale keys) is the same shape of problem: the file wins over the jar, so reworded lines never reach players. Fix: reset only the bad knob, warn on unknown keys, delete the override. Half a day. (OP-10, CF-3, OP-11)

6. **The server is one person deep, and that person's PC holds 45 unpushed commits.** Every deploy, rollback, restart, backup check, de-op and LuckPerms change needs the owner's panel key and the owner's yes. No co-owner has panel access or a written set of pre-approved emergency actions. Master is 45 commits ahead of GitHub and 81 commits ahead of the last recorded deploy; no backup restore has ever been done end to end; the deploy script's restart path has no retry and no rollback flag; nothing alerts anyone to a crash, a stuck backup or a lag spike. Fix: push now, name a co-owner with a panel sub-user, write the one page "owner away" runbook, do one restore drill. A day. (OP-1, OP-2, OP-3, OP-4, OP-16)

7. **The race lock on the War Pick does not cover mining, and the generator would silently strip the pick's mining.** Anyone of any race can craft a Dwarf's rank III War Pick and own a free, unbreakable, diamond tier pickaxe that takes Fortune III, Tunnel III and Silk Touch; only hitting is race locked. Nether gold ore is on the Dwarf Fortune list at about 10 Sovereigns a block, nine blocks a swing. And the committed War Pick JSON is out of sync with `gen_weapons.py`: the documented "never hand edit, re-run the script" workflow would ship a pick that mines like a bare hand. Also the Oathblade puts Weakness I on every hit, which halves every other melee race's damage in a duel. Half a day for all four. (FO-2, FO-12, FO-1, FO-5)

8. **Wild dungeons are invisible to players and expensive for the server.** Nothing announces them; the entrance is a bedrock cube to Bedrock players and a jigsaw block to Java. Meanwhile the top-up loop generates up to 24 overworld chunks synchronously every five seconds until 30 ruins stand, a first entry stamps a whole dungeon (tens of thousands of blocks) in one tick on everyone's thread, the Charter claims check is off unless `wildClaimsSource` is set (default empty; live value unverified), a clean restart destroys every item lying inside a half-cleared dungeon, and the armour trim bonuses meant for dungeons apply to the whole server (`trimBonusDungeonOnly` false). A tier 1 clear pays about 230 Sovereigns of gold on a shared cooldown with no per-player limit. Two to three days. (PD-W1 to W5, PD-W7, PD-W9, UX-30)

9. **A crash rolls kingdom state back while money and history move on.** Charter saves only on vanilla autosave and stop; a watchdog kill loses up to five minutes of joins, claims and keeps while Charter Currency has already taken the 32 Sovereigns for the keep and `history.jsonl` records it. Currency's 15 second write debounce has the mirror flaw: items are handed out first, so a crash inside the window gives the player the goods and the old balance. The host has 27 out-of-memory JVM crash logs on the build machine alone. Half a day each. (CF-2, EC-6)

10. **The documentation disagrees with itself, Charter Currency has no gametests, and Lemon has nothing to say.** Axiom is "enabled" in two guides and "disabled" in the newest handoff; operator lists differ; the deploy quirks described no longer exist. The money mod (Bank, Mint, shop, the coin-to-ingot recipe block) is proven only by pure arithmetic tests; nothing runs it in a server. Lemon answers every player's `/lemon` question with "I do not know that one yet." while journaling what they typed, per account, with no retention. Hide Lemon, write a `docs/README.md`, add four Currency gametests. A day. (OP-19, EC-7, LM-1, LM-3)

## Verdict

**The best thing about this suite** is the engineering discipline underneath it. Charter runs every consequential change through one engine with pure check-and-apply actions, live grant chains and a fuzz test that fingerprints the whole state; Charter Currency keeps one locked ledger and one discipline for every item path (take items, then credit; prove space, then charge); Foundry's mechanic engine is a closed registry with hard caps and server authoritative cooldowns; Pocket Dungeons' wild layer is small, pure where it can be, and writes down every decision. Bedrock was designed for, not patched: native forms, action bars, end rods, tap to cast, skin looks. Every gametest class is registered and every suite is green. Few hobby servers are built this carefully.

**The biggest risk** is that the care went into the engine and not into what players meet first. The newcomer path, the war rules, the economy's scale and the entity protection gaps were each built right and then pointed at the wrong target, and they will be met by 85 mostly new players in the first week, with the owner as the only person who can change anything.

**The single change that would improve the player experience the most** is the first ten minutes: a Charter welcome title that names the lectern, two reworded lines, a kingdom chooser that shows the ruler, the member count and one line of race traits, and letting an applicant play in the wild while they wait for a Lord. It is a day's work, it reaches Bedrock, and every other system in the suite depends on players getting through that door.
