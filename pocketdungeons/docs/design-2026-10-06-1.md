# Design pass 2026-10-06-1: boards, the Store, storage, doors, diaries

Status: **approved** by the owner (Q1 to Q7, every string), then partly
superseded by `docs/plan-2026-10-06-2.md`: item 4 (the Store) is replaced by
vanilla trade screens, item 5 (storage) amended, item 6 (resource floors) done
by that plan's sections G and B, and the boards (items 1 and 2) amended in its
"Changes to plan 2026-10-06-1". Source
session: `docs/playtests/2026-10-06-1.md` (its "Design decisions" section is
law here). Bugs: PD-149 (reopened), PD-157 to PD-160 in
`docs/reference/BUGS.md`; PD-153 is superseded.

Each item: what the player sees, the exact strings, edge cases, what was
rejected and why, then a recommendation. Open questions are marked **Q** and
collected at the end so they can be answered in one pass.

Two findings came out of reading the code that change the brief:

- **PD-160 is probably not the connector.** `flooded_hall` stamps its own
  "containment" iron doors in every open doorway (`TraversalSpecs.floodedHall`),
  each with a stone button on the *inside only*. Anyone arriving from outside
  meets an iron door with no opener on their side, which is exactly the report.
  The connector lever (`ConnectorStamper.applyIronDoor`) is still on the frame.
  Item 9 designs for both; Phase 2 confirms with a gametest first.
- **Identical doors only exist because resource floors deal step 0.** Every
  other deal hands the three doors `+1`, `+2`, `+3` in a shuffle, so no two
  doors can match. Item 6 (resource floors pay scrap) removes the zero step and
  with it the identical deal. Item 3 still adds the affix reroll the owner
  asked for, for repeated floors.

## 1. The door board

### Layout

Two text displays, the way the history board already works:

| Display | Scale | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| Title | 2.0 (today's size) | line 1 only |
| Body | 1.6 (20 percent smaller) | every other line |

The title hangs at the top of the two-row backdrop and the body centres in
what is left below it. At 1.6 a line is 0.4 blocks tall, so the usual three
body lines plus the title use 1.7 of the 2 blocks. Lines are capped at 200 px,
which at 1.6 is the 8 block backdrop exactly; every line below measures under
that (the longest, the Mineshaft line, is about 178 px).

Colours do the grouping, no labels:

| Line | Colour |
|---|---|
| Title: `DUNGEON · floor N of M` | yellow |
| Floor name | white |
| Affixes, after the name | magenta |
| What you get (scrap, ore, promises, finish) | cyan |
| `loot ×N` | green |
| The door's price | yellow |
| Light warning | gold (dark), gray (dim) |

Separators are ` · ` everywhere. `×` is the multiplication sign, which the
default font has.

### Rendered examples

A story floor with a promise, on a side branch (the owner's skeleton; Infestation's real
side edge, `silverfish_nests` to `hollow_walls`, costs 1 shard):

```
      INFESTATION · floor 3 of 4
     Hollow Walls · Overclocked
       3 scrap · 2 echo shards
     loot ×3 · costs 1 echo shard
```

A free main path door, no affix yet (compass under 5):

```
        OSSUARY · floor 1 of 3
            Charnel Steps
                1 scrap
                loot ×3
```

A resource floor after item 6, palette simplified:

```
       MINESHAFT · floor 2 of 3
   The Main Drift · Swarming
  2 scrap · coal · copper · iron · gold
                loot ×3
                  dim
```

The final floor:

```
       INFESTATION · floor 4 of 4
    The Queen's Nest · Molten
     3 scrap · 1 echo shard · vault
                loot ×3
```

The final floor of a dungeon you have never finished (first clear pays the
page; only shown while it is still owed):

```
     3 scrap · 1 echo shard · vault · diary page
```

A floor too easy for you:

```
       MINESHAFT · floor 1 of 3
              Mine Mouth
    too easy · coal · copper · iron
                loot ×3
```

### Details

- **The palette list.** Take the dungeon's `nodePalette` in authored order,
  drop the namespace, strip a leading `deepslate_` and a trailing `_ore`,
  replace `_` with a space, dedupe. Mineshaft's eight blocks become
  `coal · copper · iron · gold`. A block that is not an ore keeps its name
  (`oak log`, `warped stem`). The palette shows on every floor of a resource
  dungeon; a story dungeon's palette (Act 4 stems) does not show, it is
  scenery there.
  - Shorter: `coal · copper · iron · gold`. Clearer: `coal, copper, iron and
    gold ore`. **I prefer the shorter**: the dungeon is called Mineshaft, the
    line is cyan like everything you take home, and "gold" beside "scrap"
    reads as what the floor gives. The owner's "say gold ore" was about not
    saying it twice; the dedupe answers that.
- **Too easy.** The scrap shown is what the *owner* would bank
  (`IntervalBanking.effectiveScrap`), so a partial discount just shows the
  smaller number (`1 scrap`). When it reaches zero the scrap slot reads
  `too easy` in gray, and the rest of the line (ore, promises) follows.
  Shorter: `too easy`. Clearer: `no scrap: too easy`. **I prefer the
  shorter**: position says it is the scrap slot, and "too easy" is the reason
  a player can act on.
- **`loot ×N`** counts the floor's completion rolls:
  `Omen.baseRewardChests()` plus the zone's depth bonus for the floor count
  this door would reach (`ZoneRules.bonusChests`). The finish vault is not in
  N; it is the word `vault` on the cyan line, because its rolls are a
  different (top) tier. The loot tier word (`modest`, `fair`, `rich`,
  `lavish`) is dropped, as the skeleton drops it.
- **The price.** A free door shows nothing after `loot ×N`.
  **Q1:** the skeleton reads `loot x3 · 2 echo shards` with the same words as
  the promise line above it, once as a gain and once as a price, told apart
  only by colour. I recommend `costs 2 echo shards` on the price (one word,
  and a colourblind player cannot misread it). Shorter alternative: keep
  `2 echo shards` in yellow as approved.
- **Final floor title.** `floor 4 of 4` rather than `final floor`: the same
  shape on every door, and the `vault` on the cyan line already says it is
  the end. Alternative: `INFESTATION · final floor`.
- **Other dungeons.** The Endless Mine has no M: `ENDLESS MINE · floor 6`. An
  operator's experimental door adds `experimental` in red as its own body
  line. The first-timer `Pull the lever to descend!` and Lemon's bias note
  stay as extra body lines.
- **Removed:** `Same as door n` (PD-153 superseded), `Rewards:`,
  `Resources:`, `Loot:`, `Cost:`, `floors remaining`.

## 2. The GO HOME board

### Where carried scrap lives

On the GO HOME board only, as the owner chose. I looked for a reason to also
put it on the door board and found one weak one (a door that would *complete*
a chart is worth knowing), but the GO HOME board hangs on the same wall as
the doors, so the player sees both at the moment they decide. The door
board stays quiet. **Q2** below offers the one hint I would add if the owner
wants it.

### Layout

Same split as the door board: a title display and a body display at 80
percent of the title. The body fits the 3 by 3 panel (the longest line is
about 2.2 blocks at the current 0.8 scale).

The owner's version:

```
         GO HOME
     7 scrap carried
take home: 1 chart · 2 scrap stays
```

The shorter version I recommend:

```
         GO HOME
         7 scrap
   1 chart · 2 scrap lost
```

Why: "carried" and "take home" are labels the position already carries (the
top number is what you hold, the bottom line is what the lever does with it).
And "stays" suggests the scrap is waiting for you somewhere; it is not, it is
gone. "lost" is the honest word. Colours: `7 scrap` cyan (the same cyan as
the door board's scrap), `1 chart` green, `2 scrap lost` gray.

### Every case

| Carried | Body |
|---|---|
| 0 | `no scrap yet` (gray) |
| 3 | `3 scrap` / `2 more for a chart` (gold) |
| 5 | `5 scrap` / `1 chart` |
| 7 | `7 scrap` / `1 chart · 2 scrap lost` |
| 10 | `10 scrap` / `2 charts` |

- Under five, the useful sentence is the goal, not the loss: `2 more for a
  chart`. Alternative: `3 scrap lost`. **I prefer the goal**; it tells the
  player why to take one more door, and it still implies nothing comes home.
- **Dungeon cleared:** the title `GO HOME` turns green (today it reads
  `DUNGEON CLEARED: GO HOME`). The floor-clear title already announced the
  clear; the board only needs to say what is left to do.
- **Party:** the board is one entity seen by everyone, so it shows the
  owner's numbers, as today. Each member still reads their own result in
  chat when they bank. The chat line changes `scrap stayed in the dungeon`
  to `scrap lost` to match.

## 3. No identical doors

### What makes doors identical today

`TripDoors.dealNext` (and `dealFirst`) shuffle `+1`, `+2`, `+3` across the
three doors, so two doors can only match when the step is forced: a resource
dungeon deals every door step 0. Then a node with one edge out (Mineshaft's
`main_drift` has only `deep_face`) shows the same floor three times at the
same level, with the same seeded affixes (`AffixMath.seededFor(owner,
level)` depends only on the owner and the level).

### Design

1. **Item 6 makes the steps distinct.** Resource floors deal `+1`, `+2`, `+3`
   like every other floor, so the three doors always differ in scrap and in
   level (and so in mob strength).
2. **A repeated floor rerolls its affixes.** When the same floor stands
   behind more than one door, the second and third copies draw their seeded
   affixes again from a different seed stream, until the set differs from
   every earlier copy's. Same affix *count* (the level decides how many), a
   different *pick*. A node's signature affix stays on every copy.
3. **Seeded and stable.** The reroll seed is
   `TripDoors.seed(owner, dungeon, node, pathLength, stream)` with the door
   slot in the stream, so preview and commit compute the same set every time.
   The door carries a `variant` (0 for the first copy) and every place that
   turns an offer into affixes goes through one helper
   (`Keystone.dealtAffixes`) so the commit cannot re-add the original seeded
   set on top of the reroll. Today four call sites union
   `AffixMath.effective` with `offer.affixes()`; that union is the trap.
4. **Exhaustion.** Under compass 25 the pool has seven affixes (Feral leaves
   it on a dark floor) and the count is 1, so three distinct picks always
   exist. If a pool ever cannot produce a distinct set, the copy keeps its
   step difference and the test below flags the data.
5. **Test.** A plain-javac test deals every node of every shipped dungeon for
   a few hundred owners and asserts no two doors share floor, step, cost and
   affixes.

**Q3: below compass 5 there are no affixes to reroll.** Repeated floors then
differ only by step (1, 2 or 3 scrap). That is a real choice, so I recommend
leaving it. The alternative, giving the repeat one affix, would make new
players face affixes early on the doors that happen to repeat, and a harder
copy of the same floor with no extra reward is a door nobody should pick.

Rejected: rerolling the floor (the graph has one edge; there is nothing
else to show), and a "Same as door n" label (the owner rejected it live).

## 4. The Store

### What is broken (PD-159)

- **Dirty stacks.** `MerchantGui.onTrade` calls `cleanResult` on the output
  slot's stack *after* the take, so it cleans the next result, not the one in
  the player's hand. The bought oak log keeps the listing's name and lore and
  will not stack. (`StoreNPC.sell`, which also skips the clean, is only
  called by gametests.)
- **Stock lost on left-click.** The trade output is predicted by the client.
  The stock is claimed in `onTrade`, then the offer is replaced in the list
  (`merchant.getOffers().set`) while the screen still holds the old one. I
  cannot read SGUI 2.1's merchant source from here (the maven host is blocked
  in this environment), so I cannot prove the path. That is part of the
  argument below.

### Recommendation: a one-row shop, no trading screen

The owner wants to stop leaning on vanilla trades, and the honest-merchant
alternative needs a fix I cannot verify against code I cannot read. A custom
screen is less code than a correct merchant screen: SGUI's `SimpleGui`
(already used by `RoomBuilderCommands` and `RoomEditorKit`) with click
callbacks, where nothing can ever be picked up.

What the player sees, on right-clicking the vendor:

```
┌ Bone Collector ─────────────────────────────────────┐
│      [bread] [oak log] [arrow] [shears] [iron sword] │
│      [potion] [diamond]                              │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  (the player's own pack below, as under any chest)
```

One row (`GENERIC_9x1`), the stock centred, at most seven lines (the most
`StorePricing.composition` rolls). Each item is the real item at count 1.

Hovering an item:

```
Oak Log
4 bones
12 left
```

- Line 2 is the price, gold when you can pay, red when you cannot. Clearer
  alternative: `costs 4 bones`. **I prefer `4 bones`**: under an item's name
  a number of coins can only be its price.
- Line 3 is the stock, gray. Clearer alternative: `12 in stock`. Prefer
  `12 left`.
- There is no wallet row. The player's own inventory is on screen right under
  the shop, and the red price already answers "can I afford it".

What a click does:

| Input | Result |
|---|---|
| Any click on an item (left, right, shift, number key, Q) | buys one |
| Click when short of the currency | villager "no" sound, nothing changes |
| Click on a sold out line | villager "no" sound |
| Click anywhere else | nothing |

A purchase, in one server tick: check the line's live stock, count the
currency anywhere in the pack's 36 slots, take the price, deliver a **clean**
stack (no name, no lore, durability capped) with `Payout.deliver` (full pack:
it drops at the player's feet, as every reward does), save the stock one
lower, journal `shop_purchase`, play the villager "yes" sound, repaint the
line. Nothing ever sits on the cursor, so left-click and right-click cannot
differ. A sold out line turns into a light gray glass pane named
`sold out`. No chat lines: the screen and the sounds carry it.

Both PD-159 bugs go away by construction: the delivered stack is built fresh
and never carries the listing's display, and stock is only claimed on the
same tick the item is handed over.

**Q4:** should shift-click buy more than one (say, up to 8, or as many as
you can pay for)? Blocks sell 16 to 32 deep at one per click. I recommend one
per click for now and watching whether it annoys.

Rejected:
- *Keep the vanilla trading screen and fix it.* The cheap half (build the
  offer's result without the name and lore, show stock only as the red cross)
  fixes the dirty stack, but leaves the stock loss unexplained, and the
  output-slot flow is what the owner now wants to leave.
- *A double chest with prices as separate "coin" items.* More to read, no
  more information.

Note: PD-142 had moved the Store *onto* the vanilla trading screen at the
owner's request (2026-10-05). This reverses that, on the owner's newer wish.
PD-142's other half (the vendor named for what he buys, the stock cleaned of
lore) stays.

## 5. Run storage as a container

### Design: a private chest that never empties itself

- **Where:** the ender chest set into every staging room wall, and any other
  ender chest in the dungeon, as today. Never from survival: the dungeon pack
  never touches the survival inventory, and storage is part of the dungeon.
- **Persists:** across floors, trips, runs, logouts and restarts. It is the
  player's, not the run's: saved in `DungeonLog` per player, the same way the
  dungeon pack (`OrphanRecord`) already is.
- **Capacity:** 27 slots, the vanilla ender chest.
- **The auto-return goes away.** Teardown and logout no longer move anything.
  `RunStorage.returnAll`, the PD-150 offline notice and its `storage_returns`
  sidecar retire.
- **A failed run** (omen death): the storage rolls back to what it held when
  the trip began, the same as the pack, as it does today. Without that, the
  move on a dying run is to stash every pickup and fail for free. What was
  stored on earlier trips is safe.
- **Party:** each member has their own.
- **Name:** the menu title. Shorter: `Storage`. Clearer: `Dungeon Storage`.
  **I prefer `Dungeon Storage`**: the block *is* an ender chest, and the
  player must not think this is their vanilla one. `Run Storage` is wrong
  now that it outlives the run.
- **Migration:** anything in a live run's storage when the new build loads
  moves into the new storage, not the pack.

### In scope now

Yes, about a day: a codec field on `DungeonLog.Entry` (the item list codec
`OrphanRecord` uses), `RunStorage.onUse` opening a container backed by it,
the trip-start snapshot kept as today, and the teardown call removed. The
risk is duplication on a race between the snapshot rollback and an open
menu, so the rollback closes any open storage menu first. It stays last in
Phase 2.

## 6. Resource floors pay scrap

### Now

`TripDoors.dealFirst` and `dealNext` drop the `resource ? 0 :` special case:
a resource floor deals 1 to 3 scrap like any floor and banks through
`IntervalBanking` unchanged. D12 changes to "pays scrap and what you mine; no
shard, no vault, diary page on the first finish". The door board shows scrap
and ore (item 1).

### Fixed floor levels: defer

Floor level is `compass + step`, so a solo player is never above a floor and
the too-easy discount never bites on their own. Authored fixed levels would
make it real, but they touch difficulty (mob scale), affix count, loot band
clamps and the history board, and they contradict D10 ("the keystone sets
difficulty"). Defer, and record it in `DUNGEON_STRUCTURE_DESIGN.md` as a
follow-up.

If a Mineshaft clear turns out too rich (the session's open question: about
2 charts a clear), the cheap lever is a per-dungeon **level ceiling**
(`levelCap` in the dungeon file: floor level is `min(compass + step,
cap)`), one field and one `Math.min`. A compass 11 player in a Mineshaft
capped at 8 would get `too easy` on most doors. **Q5:** ship scrap without a
ceiling and watch, as I recommend, or ship the ceiling with it?

## 7. Ore hidden in walls

### How it works in `mineshaft_seam`

The narrow rooms are solid rock either side of a 3 wide path. At stamp time,
after the template and the declared `nodes`, the stamper picks a few pockets
**inside** that rock: positions whose six neighbours are all solid interior
blocks (never shell, never a face touching air). Each pocket is 1 to 3 ore
blocks of the dungeon's palette, registered as nodes like any declared node,
so `nodes_total` and `nodes_mined` count them and the journal sees them. The
player finds one by mining into a wall, exactly what the session showed they
already do.

- **How many:** seeded per room. Default 0 to 2 pockets (about 1.2 on average),
  each with the same per-ore chances PD-155 set. The visible seam keeps its
  current numbers.
- **Never in the shell.** The shell rule already refuses the break, but a
  pocket there would be a promise the player cannot collect.
- **Never visible.** A pocket that would touch air is skipped, not moved.

### Does it generalise

Yes, as a dungeon field rather than a room field: `hiddenOre` on the
dungeon (pockets per room, pocket size, which palette blocks). Every room the
dungeon stamps with solid interior rock gets it, generic halls included.
Mineshaft first; the Endless Mine is the natural second. Story dungeons stay
without it, since their walls are not meant as a mine.

**Cheap: yes.** One pass over the interior after stamping, reusing the node
registration that exists.

## 8. Diary books (PD-157)

### Pagination

`DiaryDelivery.book` paginates each authored page into real book pages:

- A book page holds 14 lines of 114 px. Width is measured with a table of
  the default font's advances (most letters 6 px, `i` and `.` 2, `l` 3, `t`
  4, space 4); the budget is **13 lines**, one spare, because the server
  cannot measure the client font exactly.
- **Split at a paragraph break first** (`\n\n`), then at a sentence end,
  then at a word. A new page never starts with a blank line.
- **An authored page always starts a new book page.** The split only ever
  adds pages.

My estimate finds six entries over the limit (1, 2, 6, 8, 15, 16) and four at
exactly 14 lines (5, 7, 9, 13). Entry 8 becomes:

```
Page 1                         Page 2
The first cave I found         I took what the walls
had timbers in it.             gave me. It felt like
                               borrowing.
Old planks. Fence posts.
A rail running off into
the dark.

He had built this. Years
ago, when he still knew
what a mine was for.
```

### The found-copy shuffle

The shuffle moves **authored pages**, each keeping its split pages together
and in order. "Found out of order" is the fiction for pages torn from a
diary; a sentence that stops at the bottom of one page and resumes three
pages later is not a jumbled diary, it is a broken one.

Books already found keep their clipped pages; the Diaries menu reads the
authored text and shows it whole. Rebuilding books held in packs is not worth
the risk. A unit test runs every shipped entry through the paginator and
fails on any page over budget.

## 9. Iron doors (PD-160)

### Rule

**Every iron door opens from the side you arrive on.** No connector is ever
locked by design. A lock belongs to a room (`access: gated`), where the
room's puzzle is the key and the existing solvability pass guarantees it.

### Room doors: `flooded_hall`

Its containment doors keep their inside button, and gain a stone pressure
plate on the outside, in the doorway slot of the neighbouring cell (that slot
is wall plane, so neighbouring room content cannot land on it). Walking in
opens the door; it shuts behind you and the water stays in. Edge: a drowned
stepping on the outer plate opens the door for a second. Acceptable.

Phase 2 starts with a gametest: stamp `flooded_hall` beside a plain hall
and check every doorway has an opener on both faces. If the live report was
something else, this item is revisited before any change.

### Connector doors

- **Near side** (the side nearer the entrance, the one that stamps the
  door): the lever moves off the door frame to a seeded spot on another wall
  of the same cell, at a solid, dry block with air in front. This answers
  PD-55 ("switches should be on other walls not attached to the door")
  without leaving the room, so PD-27's guarantee holds. If no such spot
  exists (a flooded or packed room), it falls back to the frame.
- **Far side:** a stone button on the frame, so backtracking works. PD-62's
  "place your own redstone" exemption stays as a second way.
- **A closed iron door explains itself** when right-clicked, on the action
  bar, once per few seconds:
  - connector door: `The lever is in this room.` (clearer) or
    `Find the lever.` (shorter). **I prefer the clearer one**: the fear is
    "is this broken", and it answers that.
  - gated room door: `Opens when the room is solved.`

Rejected: the lever in a chest (inventory juggling for no puzzle), and the
lever in another cell (PD-55 option 3: needs the connector wired into the
`provides: redstone` solvability system; worth it only if the near-wall
lever proves too easy).

**Q6:** is a lever on another wall of the same room the challenge you want,
or should connector doors stop being obstacles at all (plates both sides)?

## 10. Smaller items

- **PD-158, the omen bar.** The title prints the floor's omen (`Omen 4/4`)
  but the fill is the trip's omen sum against the next band
  (`Omen.bandProgress`), so they disagree. The floor omen is the one that
  kills you: a death adds 1 and 4 ends the run. Design: fill is
  `floor omen / 4`, colour follows it (0 to 1 green, 2 to 3 yellow, 4 red),
  and `one more fall ends the run` shows from omen 3, not from trip band 2 as
  now. The band still settles the bank; it no longer drives the bar.
- **PD-149.** No design question, a repro. Lead: both live misses were
  floor 1 or 2 with only generic halls, and `ensureResourceRooms` gives up
  silently when `forceRoom` finds no cell whose mask and role fit a
  mineshaft room. Phase 2 logs that case, replays the live seed, and checks
  that the floor-1 plan really carries `Floor.resource`.
- **Diamond pickaxe durability.** Tool caps today: wood and gold 8, stone and
  copper 12, iron 16, diamond 20, netherite 24. Diamond is 6 times iron in
  vanilla and only 1.25 times here. **Q7:** proposed diamond 32 and
  netherite 40, iron unchanged.
