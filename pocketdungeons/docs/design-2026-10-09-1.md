# Design brief 2026-10-09-1: eleven open design questions from playtest 2026-10-08-1

This document is a prompt. It is written to be read with no other context. Read it all, then answer
the questions in the output format at the end. **Do not write code.** The owner will paste your reply
back to a coding agent that carries it out, so every answer must be specific enough to build from.

House rule for everything you write: never use an em dash or a spaced double hyphen as punctuation.
Use a colon, semicolon, comma, parentheses or a new sentence instead. (Flags like `--stacktrace` and a
single hyphen in a compound word are fine.)

## The project in two sentences

Pocket Dungeons is a Fabric mod for Minecraft 26.2: a lobby with a merchant row, a Librarian and a
salvage station, and a run of procedurally planned dungeon floors made of hand-built rooms, played
solo or in a party of up to four. It is a roguelite loop (go in, clear floors, bank or lose what you carry,
raise your compass, go deeper) with a scarcity rule: resources such as wood, ore, food and sand are meant
to be finite, so mechanics should cost time, position, lives or risk, never stacks of items.

## The model you are designing inside (settled, do not reopen)

Read `docs/decision-2026-10-07-haul-and-blood-doors.md` first; it is the current rule set. In short:

- **Compass** = the permanent level (only rises). **Scrap** fills it: 5 scrap per compass level
  (`ScrapMath.SCRAP_PER_CHART`). **Haul** = scrap carried on the current trip, at risk.
- A cleared floor pays its dealt step (1 to 3 scrap) into each member's haul at or above their compass,
  1 below it (`ScrapMath.floorPay`). Going home or finishing banks 100 percent; a failed dungeon (fifth
  death, or a quit, which counts as a fail) banks `failHaulKeepPercent` (50) and loses the rest.
- **Lives**: 5 per trip; a death costs one; side doors ("blood doors") cost 1 life (2 in Frostworks).
- A trip is **one dungeon**. A dungeon is a graph of floors (nodes, `src/main/resources/data/pocketdungeons/dungeon/*.json`).
  A finish pays emeralds, a vault and a diary page.
- Naming is settled: the player-facing words are compass, scrap, haul, lives, charts. Do not introduce
  older words (keystone, chart scrap).

## The player (quote him, design for him)

One live playtester (MrPinoy123456789, who is also the owner), solo, four hours, keystone 5 to 10, three
full dungeon finishes. His habits matter:

- "I never read any of the run messages in chat, I usually use them as a log if I think I've missed some
  information." Critical information must live on titles, the omen bar, boards or a persistent display.
- Wants Darkest Dungeon style risk: failing costs progress but not everything.
- Likes rooms that tell a story, verticality, one clear idea per room, the minecraft-SCP anomaly tone.
- Verdicts this session: "The shop works perfectly", "new relay room is good", "this is probably the
  coolest room so far" (copper_works_forge), "It was definitely pressure" (lives at 2), "I felt like I
  had just barely enough resources to succeed" (Spawner Dungeon capstone at 1 life).

## The content that exists (so you do not invent what is already there)

19 dungeons by act: Act 1: Mineshaft (4 floors), Infestation (6 nodes), Ossuary (5), Rootworks (6),
Spawner Dungeon (capstone, 4), Endless Mine (endless, 1). Act 2: Copper Works (6), Deepslate (9),
Frostworks (9), Cow Pits (2), Ancient City (capstone, 6). Act 3: Prismarine (6), Drowned Vault
(capstone, 7). Act 4: Basalt Foundry (7), Blackstone (8), Wither Keep (capstone, 6). Act 5: Ender Archive
(6), The End (7), Herobrine (capstone, 5). Every Act 1 and 2 dungeon and floor now has an authored
`notes` line on the door board (the reason to choose that door). A floor soak found no hard defects in
Acts 1 and 2. Rooms are Java `RoomSpec` plus a JSON manifest; ordeals are in `docs/reference/ORDEALS.md`
(the Plate Relay replaced `hold_the_plate`).

## Hard constraints on every option

1. **Scarcity.** A mechanic should cost time, position, lives or risk. Do not require crafted or keyed
   items to finish a floor (soft-lock risk; see the ominous-key toll, PD-170).
2. **Risk is real.** A system where only emeralds are wagered was rejected.
3. **Legibility.** A rule the player cannot see "feels like it doesn't exist". Say where each number is
   shown (item lore, title, omen bar, board, display) and how the player learns it. Boards read tersely:
   few words, no jargon, colour does the grouping. Give a short and a clear version of any new text and
   say which you prefer.
4. **Server side only.** There is no client mod. HUD-like output is limited to titles, subtitles, the
   action bar, boss/omen bars, scoreboard sidebar, chat, dialogs (the mod has dialog screens), item lore,
   and in-world text displays.
5. **Feasibility.** The owner took "not feasible currently" options at their word before; propose the
   nearest feasible version instead. Say how hard each option is to build here (small, medium, large).
6. **Party and ominous.** Say how each option scales for 1 to 4 players and for ominous/affixed floors.
7. Things the owner ruled out or deferred stay that way: a quit is a fail; the haul model above;
   the player does not craft keys to progress.

## The eleven questions

For each, the report quote and its PD entry in `docs/reference/BUGS.md` (section "2026-10-09") are given.
Read the report `docs/playtests/2026-10-08-1.md` for the surrounding session.

### Q1 (PD-181, High): choosing the act and dungeon instead of three random doors

> "The 3 random doors doesn't really work with the new dungeon act system" ...
> "players should be able to choose the act and dungeon" ... first thing he'd change: "Redesigning the
> dungeon selection."

Today: at the start of a trip the player sees up to three offered doors dealt from unlocked dungeons
(`Keystone.offers`, `DungeonCommands` reroll, `RunLifecycle` door commit at lines near 443 and 566, the
first deal salted by `IntervalState.doorReroll`; the Spawner Dungeon is forced as the first door for new
players). Inside a trip, per-floor doors are dealt by the dungeon graph (`TripDoors`, `DoorAffixes`).
Open decisions: the picker UI (a dialog screen? a board? a book?), what is shown per dungeon (act, floors,
status uncleared/cleared/capstone, the `notes` line, ore/loot promise, recommended compass), how acts
unlock (`ActProgress`, `DungeonProgress`), what happens to the echo shard and "Same as door" affix
mechanics and the reroll, how a party picks (owner only? vote?), what stops the player always farming the
easiest dungeon (compass gating, scrap scaling from Q2, diminishing pay for cleared dungeons?), and what
happens to randomness (should anything stay random: affixes, floor order within a dungeon, room draws?).

### Q2 (PD-182): a scrap curve

> "make it so each level requires more scrap than the previous and then make higher level floors reward
> more scrap appropriately."

Today: 5 scrap per level at every level; floors pay a dealt step of 1 to 3, at or above compass, else 1.
Decide: the cost curve (a formula or a table; what does compass 1, 10, 25, 50 cost?), the pay curve by
floor level and dungeon act/tier (and by floor depth within a dungeon), how it interacts with the
overlevelled-pay rule, the fail keep percent and the Q1 picker, and the migration for existing players
(their compass must never drop; their bar must stay valid). Keep banked-bar display legible (the compass
item lore and omen bar show `3/5`-style progress).

### Q3 (PD-183): one stealth language for sculk, and replace `sensor_gallery`

> "I don't like how these sculks act differently then the ones that spawn enemies" ... "this room needs
> to be replaced with something else" ... "sculk-like things like the shrieker and sensor should read as
> stealth mechanics."

Today: sensors in a room feed the omen/pressure (`PressureSources`, `SculkOmen`: sensor pulses count
toward the omen, Ancient City arms all sculk) while shriekers and the Spawner Dungeon brood chamber
spawn mobs; the Ancient City is the Act 2 capstone. Decide: one rule that sensors, shriekers and catalysts all
obey (what does being loud cost; what does being quiet earn), how the player learns it (sound, particle,
title, bar), what the Ancient City's capstone does with it, and a replacement room concept for
`sensor_gallery` (one clear idea, built on the rule, no required items). Give 2 or 3 room concepts and
recommend one.

### Q4 (PD-184): a finale on the last floor

> "There wasn't a boss for copper works" ... "There should be some extra challenge in the last floor."
> He was asked boss or horde and did not answer.

Today: capstones (Spawner Dungeon brood wave, Ancient City, Drowned Vault, Wither Keep, Herobrine) have
fights (`CapstoneFights`, `BroodWave`, `WitherFight`, `HerobrineFight`); ordinary dungeons end on a normal
floor with a final room (`docs/reference/FINAL_FLOOR_ROOMS.md`). Decide: what the finale is for a normal dungeon
(a per-dungeon miniboss, a horde wave on the final room, a capstone ordeal in the final node, or a mix per
act), how it differs per dungeon theme, how it scales with party size and lives, what it pays, and the
build cost. The Spawner Dungeon brood fight at 1 life was "just barely enough resources"; use that as the
target pressure.

### Q5 (PD-185): Copper Works mobs wear copper gear

> "could we thematically make it so that all mobs wear two pieces of copper, including weapons?" ... "just
> in this copper dungeon."

Decide what "two pieces" means (copper armour pieces exist in 26.2; copper tools/weapons may too: say
what to use), drop chances (must not become a free gear farm; scarcity), whether it is spawner mobs only,
how it reads (the board notes line? a title?), and whether the pattern generalises into a per-dungeon
"mob uniform" field so later dungeons (Frostworks: stray and leather in ice colours?) can opt in.

### Q6 (PD-186): the hopper-key toll room

> Worst room of the night: "The room with the key in the hopper to open the iron doors."

Today: `SpurToll` HOPPER_KEY rooms (`barred_vault`, `ominous_bargain`) and the Frame Lock room all ask the
player to drop a specific item into a hopper to open an iron door. Decide which to keep, rework or retire;
whether the fault is readability, the fiction, or the room; and propose a replacement mechanic or fiction
that keeps "a vault that needs a key from this floor" (ominous trial keys are a real economy) without a
hopper puzzle. Remember the soft-lock rule: the floor must remain finishable without the key.

### Q7 (PD-187 and PD-190): a persistent display, and moving critical info out of chat

> "Is it possible to create a HUD? ... floor information and Chart/scrap information in the top left or
> right corner" ... "persistent text should be more than enough" ... "I never read any of the run messages in chat."

Decide the server-side channel (scoreboard sidebar vs an in-world text display riding the player vs
boss-bar lines vs action bar), exactly what lines it shows (floor name and number, dungeon, lives, haul,
spawners remaining, compass bar?) and in what order and colour, when it shows or hides, how it behaves in a
party, and the cost (performance, packet rate). Then audit: list the kinds of information that are
chat-only today (floor pay, key redemption, omen notices, milestone fanfare, shop results, bank results)
and say where each should move (title, subtitle, omen bar, action bar, the display, a dialog). Chat stays
as the log.

### Q8 (PD-188): replace the Feral affix, and wolf content

> "Feral seems out of place, we should instead make a wolf themed dungeon, make a wolf/pet themed floor for
> another dungeon, and create a rare generic wolf themed room. And then create a new affix to replace feral."

Today: affixes are in `AffixIds`/`AffixEffects`/`data/.../affix`; Feral summons neutral wolves. Decide: a
wolf-themed dungeon concept (act, theme, floors, final-floor idea, what it offers a player that others do
not; remember the `notes` rule: a floor note is the reason to choose it), a wolf/pet floor inside another
dungeon (which dungeon, which node), a rare generic wolf room concept (one clear idea, no taming
requirement), and the replacement affix (name, effect, counterplay, risk/reward, ominous interplay, how it
reads on the door board). Wolves already helped in the Spawner Dungeon fight ("enough pet dogs to help with
the initial wave"); say whether pets are a feature to keep and how a player gets them without breaking
scarcity (bones are a Mineshaft/Ossuary resource).

### Q9 (PD-189): ore expectations on mine-themed floors

> "Still yet to see any hidden ores in this room." (Endless Mine) and "This room is very good, but it didn't
> have enough ore in it." (`deep_shaft_landing`)

Today: `hiddenOre` is declared by the Mineshaft only; the Endless Mine is an endless descent with its own
rules (`EndlessMineRules`); Cow Pits now promises no ore but borrows ore rooms. Decide: whether `hiddenOre`
extends to the Endless Mine (and which dungeons beyond), the ore promise each mine-flavoured dungeon makes,
whether `deep_shaft_landing` gets more ore, and the guardrail against turning every dungeon into a mine
(the scarcity rule: ore is finite per floor, and notes must not promise what the planner cannot deliver).

### Q10 (carried): small display and rule items the playtest raised

Answer briefly (a sentence or two each, with a recommendation):
a. Is there a better word or visual than "GO HOME" for the lever board once the dungeon is finished?
b. The Infestation clear title was not noticed ("No I didn't notice a Big Ingestation cleared") while the
   Spawner Dungeon one was. What distinguishes them and should a finish get a longer or louder title?
c. Should a dungeon finish still auto-bank (the instant payout he asked to keep), and is the lever then a
   pure "leave" button? What should its board say?
d. Floor identity: he asked "what's this room called?" on floor 1 although titles carry the floor name.
   Is that a titles problem or a habit problem, and does the Q7 display solve it?
e. Poison now caps at 10 seconds in dungeons (knob `poisonMaxSeconds`). Any other status effect that
   forces "hide for 30 seconds" play that should be capped the same way?

### Q11: ordering

Rank Q1 to Q10 by player impact over build cost, group them into waves a coding agent can ship one
branch at a time (each wave independently testable), and say which waves must be played live before the
next begins (for example the picker before the scrap curve, because the curve is tuned against what the
player chooses).

## Required output format

Reply as one markdown document with these sections, in this order:

1. **Summary.** Ten lines at most: the decision for each question.
2. **Per question (Q1 to Q10).** For each: (a) the problem in the player's words; (b) two or three options,
   each with what the player does, why it is interesting, party and ominous scaling, scarcity impact, and
   build size (small/medium/large); (c) **one recommendation** with the reason; (d) a spec table the coder
   can build from (numbers, field names, where each lives: config key, dungeon JSON key, constant, new
   file); (e) the exact player-facing text (short and clear versions, and which you prefer).
3. **Migration.** What existing saves and existing dungeon JSON need (compass, bar, haul, offered doors,
   already-finished dungeons).
4. **Knobs.** Every number a tuner should be able to change, with default and range, so it can be added to
   `pocketdungeons.json` or the JSON data.
5. **Waves (Q11).** The ordered list of waves, each with scope, dependencies, tests it should have, and the
   live check it owes.
6. **Open until played.** The questions only live play can answer.
7. **Supersedes.** The earlier decisions, plans and PD entries this replaces or amends, by id.

Do not write code. Do not reopen the settled model. If a question cannot be answered well without more
information, say what you need to know and give your best provisional answer anyway.
