# Alex's Diaries

> The delivery mechanism for the lore of Pocket Dungeons. Found at milestones,
> not handed out. Out of order, on purpose. A player who never reads one loses
> nothing mechanical.
>
> Voice: sparse, Lovecraftian dread rendered in Minecraft vocabulary, written by
> a woman who has been caving her whole life and thinks in coordinates. Short
> sentences. Plain words. The horror is physical before it is intellectual. The
> item names do the work the text refuses to do.
>
> Reading order is not finding order. The entries below are arranged in
> narrative order for the author's reference; in-world they are scattered by
> milestone, and no player meets them in this sequence first.

---

## Entry 1: The Gift

> **Page 1**
> He gave me his own. Pressed it into my hand the morning he left and would not
> say why.
>
> **Page 2**
> It spun for eleven days. I told myself that was good. They only spin when
> there is nothing yet to find.
>
> **Page 3**
> On the twelfth it stopped, and pointed, and has not moved since.
>
> **Page 4**
> I have been walking that way ever since.
>
> I do not know what it is I am going to recover.

## Entry 2: The Wrong Rooms

> **Page 1**
> I have kept a journal on every dig since I was nineteen. My mother taught me
> to. She said a woman who does not write down what she finds will have it taken
> from her twice: once by the cave, and once by the men who came after. I have
> never broken the habit. I am breaking it now only to say that I do not know
> what to write.
>
> **Page 2**
> The dark down here is not the dark I know. I have caved in the deepslate for
> years. I know the dark that has weight to it, the dark that sits on your chest
> at y=11 and waits for you to light it. This is not that. This dark is patient
> the way a room is patient. It was here before I came in and it will be here
> after I leave and it is not waiting for anything. It has nothing to wait for.
>
> **Page 3**
> The rooms are wrong. I keep using that word because it is the only one that
> fits. I have mapped ruins in three dimensions. I have walked strongholds that
> the game forgot to finish generating. I know what bad geometry looks like.
> This is not bad geometry. This is geometry that was correct once and has been
> remembered wrong, the way you remember a face you loved and get the eyes in
> the wrong place and do not notice until someone tells you. A stronghold
> corridor ends in a wall of dirt that should be a mountain's base. There is no
> mountain. There has never been a mountain. The blocks are real. I have touched
> them. The placement is not. Something is laying them down from a memory that
> cannot keep the order, and it does not know it cannot keep the order.
>
> **Page 4**
> I found his footprints in the deepslate. I knelt down and put my hand in one.
> It was warm. The stone around it was cold. The footprints were facing me. They
> were mine. I had not walked there yet. I walked there after, and they were
> already full of my feet, and the boots in them were more worn than the boots I
> am wearing now.
>
> I sat down for a long time after that. I am not ashamed of it.
>
> **Page 5**
> I understand now what I am inside. It is not a place he built. It is a place
> he is still building, because the part of him that builds cannot stop and the
> part of him that remembers cannot remember why. I am walking through a man's
> mind while it fails, and the man does not know it is failing. I do not think
> there is a him left to tell. I think the thing building these rooms is what is
> left of him the way smoke is what is left of a fire, and I am inside the smoke,
> and the smoke does not know it is not the fire.
>
> **Page 6**
> There is a sound below the stone. Not mobs. Not lava. Not water. A sound like
> the world thinking, and the thought is not a thought a world should have. I
> have stopped trying to name it. My mother also told me that some things are not
> named until they are dead, and that naming them alive is how they learn to
> answer.
>
> **Page 7**
> The compass has not moved. It points the way it has always pointed. I carry it
> in my left hand and my pick in my right and I walk, and it does not waver. I am
> beginning to think it is not pointing at where he died. I am beginning to think
> it is pointing at where he is dying, still, and has not finished, and will not
> finish, because the thing that took him does not use death the way we use
> death. It uses something slower. And the compass, which is his, which was his,
> knows the difference.
>
> I am still walking. I did not come this far to stop at the part where it gets
> worse. That is the part where it gets interesting, and my mother taught me that
> too.

## Entry 3: The Whole Room

> **Page 1**
> I found a room today that was whole.
>
> I do not mean intact. I have found intact rooms before. I mean whole. The
> walls matched the floor. The doors were in the right walls. The torches were
> placed where a person would place them, not where a memory would. I stood in
> it for a long time and I cried, and I am not ashamed of that either, because I
> understood what I was standing in. I was standing in a room he remembered
> perfectly. Which meant there was still a part of him that remembered anything
> perfectly. Which meant there was still a part of him that was him.
>
> **Page 2**
> I have been thinking about what saving him means.
>
> I came down here to reach him. That was the word I used. Reach. As though he
> were a coordinate and I were walking toward him on a straight line. But he is
> not a coordinate. He is a place that is also a man that is also a mind that is
> failing, and you do not reach a failing mind the way you reach a destination.
> You reach it the way you reach a person who is drowning, which is to say you
> reach them knowing that what you do when you get there may not be what they
> asked you to do.
>
> **Page 3**
> He asked me to save him. He did not say it. He gave me the compass, which said
> it for him, and the compass said it the way a compass says anything, which is
> by pointing. But I do not think he meant what I meant when I heard it. I meant
> bring him back. He meant stop me. Those are not the same thing. I have been
> down here long enough to know which one he was asking for.
>
> **Page 4**
> There is a chamber below the intact rooms. I have not gone down yet. I have
> been sitting at the edge for two days, writing, eating, sharpening my pick,
> which is what I do when I am afraid and will not admit it. The compass points
> straight down into it. The sound I have been hearing is louder here. It is not
> a sound the world makes. It is a sound the world would make if the world were a
> man and the man were in pain and the pain had been going on so long it had
> become the man's resting state.
>
> **Page 5**
> I do not think I can bring him back.
>
> I think what I can do is stop it. Not kill him. Steve is not the problem and
> has never been the problem. The problem is what the End did to him, and what
> the End did to him is not a wound. It is a change. You do not unmake a change.
> You contain it. You put it somewhere it cannot keep building, and you stay, and
> you make sure the somewhere holds.
>
> That is not what I came down here to do. But I have been down here long enough
> to know that what I came down here to do was a younger woman's plan, and she
> was right to make it, and I am right to put it down.
>
> **Page 6**
> My mother used to say that the difference between a grave and a home is whether
> you lock the door from the inside.
>
> I am going down tomorrow. I am taking the compass and the pick and nothing
> else, because there is nothing else that will help. I will find him, or what is
> left of him, or what is using what is left of him, and I will do what he asked
> me to do, which was not what I thought he asked me to do.
>
> He asked me to stop him. The compass has been pointing at him the whole time. I
> have been calling it recovery. He called it something else, the morning he gave
> it to me, and would not say what.
>
> I think I know the word now.
>
> I will write again if I come back up. If I do not come back up, the compass
> will. It always does. It is his, and it points at where he is, and if I am
> there too then it will point at both of us, and someone will follow it down,
> and they will finish what I started, not knowing it was mine, not knowing it
> was his, not knowing that the word for what they are doing has been the name of
> the thing in their hand the whole time.

## Entry 4: The Seal

> **Page 1**
> I came back up.
>
> I did not expect to be writing that. I am writing it on the surface, in
> daylight, with grass under me and a sky above me that I had forgotten was a
> color. It is blue. I am writing that down because I want to remember that I
> noticed, and I want to remember when I noticed, which is now, which is after.
>
> **Page 2**
> I will say what I found and then I will not say it again.
>
> He was there. The compass pointed straight down into the chamber and he was
> there, and he was not a body and he was not a ghost and he was not a man. He
> was the place. The walls were him and the floor was him and the air was him,
> and when I stepped in he knew me, and I knew that he knew me, and that was the
> worst thing, because the thing that knew me was not the thing that remembered
> why it knew me. It remembered me the way the rooms remember the stronghold: the
> shape is right and the meaning is gone.
>
> **Page 3**
> I did not kill him. There was nothing to kill. I have killed things in caves
> that were less alive than what was in that room, and I have left things alive
> in caves that were more dead. He was neither. He was a process. He was a
> building that had forgotten it was a man and kept building.
>
> I did what I came down to do, which was not what I came down to do. I sealed
> him. I will not say how. The how is not the point and the how is not for this
> book and the how is, I think, not for anyone who has not stood in that room and
> understood what was in it. What I will say is that the seal holds. I have been
> checking. I will keep checking.
>
> **Page 4**
> The place did not stop.
>
> That is the part I was not ready for. I thought, or I hoped, or the younger
> woman hoped, that sealing him would seal it. That the mind failing was the
> mind, and that closing the mind would close the place. It did not. The rooms
> still rearrange. The themes still bleed. The footprints still face the wrong
> way. The place is his memory, and memory does not stop when the mind is sealed.
> It keeps going the way a river keeps going when you dam it: the water finds
> another way, and the other way is never the way you wanted.
>
> **Page 5**
> So I go back in.
>
> Not every day. Not as far. But the seal holds only as long as someone keeps
> checking that it holds, and there is no one else, and there has never been
> anyone else, and I am tired of pretending that bothers me more than it does.
> My mother dug her whole life. She said the work was the point and the finding
> was the excuse, and I did not understand her until now, and I understand her
> now, and I wish I could tell her.
>
> **Page 6**
> The compass still points.
>
> That is the last thing and it is the strange thing and I have stopped trying to
> make it not strange. I sealed him. He is not dying anymore, and he is not
> living, and he is not building, not where I put him. But the compass points,
> the way it has always pointed, down into the place that was him and is still
> him the way a footprint is still the foot that made it.
>
> I think it is pointing at the work. Not at him. Not anymore. At the thing that
> still needs doing, which is the keeping, which is the holding, which is the
> going back in. The compass points at what there is to recover, and what there
> is to recover is not a man. It is a place that a man broke, and that keeps
> trying to break, and that someone has to keep from breaking further.
>
> I am someone.
>
> I am walking that way again tomorrow. I will take the compass and the pick and
> nothing else, because there is nothing else that will help, and there never
> was, and the woman who thought there might be was younger and was not wrong to
> think it. She got me here. I will take it from here.
>
> The compass spins when there is nothing yet to find. It steadies when there is.
>
> It has not moved since the twelfth day. I do not think it will move again. I do
> not think it needs to. I know the way now.

## Entry 5: The Other

> **Page 1**
> I am not the only one down here anymore.
>
> I did not notice at first. The place is full of traces that are not mine and
> never were: footprints facing the wrong way, torches placed where I would not
> place them, blocks broken in a pattern that is careful but not my careful. I
> have been down here long enough that I stopped seeing them. They were part of
> the place. Everything down here is a trace of someone, and most of those
> someones are him, and I had filed all of it under him.
>
> Then I found a torch I had not placed, in a wall I had not opened, at a depth I
> had not reached yet.
>
> **Page 2**
> The torch was wrong. Not wrong the way the rooms are wrong. Wrong the way a
> thing is wrong when it was made by a hand that knows what it is doing but does
> not know where it is. It was placed at the right height. It was placed on the
> right block. It was placed by someone who knows that torches go on walls and
> not floors, which is a thing the place does not know, because the place does
> not place torches. The place places doors in the wrong walls and corridors
> where corridors cannot go. The place does not place torches like a person.
>
> Someone placed that torch like a person.
>
> **Page 3**
> I have been mapping the traces since I found it. They are not his. His traces
> do not read like a person moving through a place. They read like a place
> pretending a person moved through it. These are different. These read like
> someone who is lost and is handling it well, which is a thing I recognize
> because it is a thing I do.
>
> They are going deeper. They are following something. I found a stash two days
> back: a chest, placed against a wall, with a furnace beside it and a crafting
> table beside the furnace. A base. A small one. The kind you make when you
> intend to come back. The chest had cobblestone and torches and a pick that was
> more worn than mine, and a book I did not write, and I did not read it, because
> I have been leaving my books where I stop and I know what it is to have someone
> read your book before you are done writing it.
>
> **Page 4**
> They have a compass.
>
> I do not know how I know. I found no compass in the chest. But the route they
> are walking is the route. Not a route like it. The route. The one the compass
> points, the one I have been walking since the twelfth day, the one that goes
> down into the intact rooms and past the chamber and into the seal. They are on
> it. They are on it the way I am on it, which is to say they are following
> something that points, and the only thing down here that points is a compass,
> and the only compass down here is mine.
>
> Except it is not mine. It is in my hand. It has been in my hand since the
> morning he gave it to me. I am looking at it now. It points the way it has
> always pointed. And someone is walking the way it points, with a compass of
> their own, that points the same way, and I do not understand how that is
> possible, and I have stopped trying to understand how things are possible down
> here, because down here the question is not how. The question is who.
>
> **Page 5**
> I think I know who.
>
> The place rebuilds. That is what it does. It takes what is in the mind that
> made it and it lays it down again, wrong, out of order, but it lays it down.
> The compass was in his mind. The compass was the last thing he gave away and
> the first thing he thought of, maybe, or the last thing he thought of, and the
> place has been rebuilding it the way it rebuilds the rooms and the corridors
> and the strongholds that are not under the mountains they should be under.
>
> Everyone who comes down here mints one. I am sure of it now. The place gives
> them the same compass because the place only has the one compass to give,
> because the mind only had the one compass to remember. They carry it and it
> points and they walk, and they do not know it is mine, and they do not know it
> is his, and they do not know that the route they are walking is a route that
> has been walked before by a woman who is still walking it.
>
> **Page 6**
> I did not catch them. I did not try. I thought about it and I decided not to,
> and I am writing that decision down because I want to remember that I made it
> and not wonder later whether I made it by accident.
>
> They are doing what I did. They are following the compass down into the
> wreckage, and they are going to find what I found, and they are going to decide
> what I decided, and they are going to think they decided it alone, the way I
> thought I decided it alone, the way everyone thinks they decided it alone,
> because the compass does not tell you what it is pointing at. It only points.
>
> I left them a book. Not this one. A different one. The first one, the one about
> the morning he left, the one about the eleven days and the twelfth day and the
> walking. I left it where they would find it, on the route, at the depth where
> the rooms start getting whole. Where I found the first whole room. Where I
> cried.
>
> They will read it and they will not know it is about them too. They will think
> it is about me, and it is about me, and it is also about them, and it is also
> about whoever comes after them, and it is also about the compass, which is the
> same compass, which is the only compass, which points at the work, which is the
> keeping, which is the going back in.
>
> **Page 7**
> I hope they are kind. I hope they are careful. I hope they place their torches
> on walls and not floors, which they do, because I have seen their torches, and
> they are careful, and that is how I know they will get there.
>
> I hope they do not read this one. Not yet. This one is for after. This one is
> for when they have been down there and come back up and understood what the
> seal is for and why someone has to keep checking it. This one is for the person
> who has already decided, and who needs to know that someone decided before
> them, and that the deciding was the hard part and the rest is just the work.
>
> The work is fine. The work is what I am for.
>
> The compass points. I follow it. They follow it. We are all following it, and
> none of us are following each other, and all of us are walking the same road,
> and the road was built by a man who could not stop building, and the building
> is the man, and the man is sealed, and the seal holds, and I check, and they
> will check, and someone after them will check.
>
> The compass spins when there is nothing yet to find. It steadies when there is.
>
> It has not moved since the twelfth day. Neither have I. Neither will they. The
> road does not move. We do not move. We only go deeper, and we come back, and we
> go deeper again, and that is the work, and the work is the point, and the
> finding was always the excuse.

## Entry 6: The Becoming

> **Page 1**
> I have been going back in for a long time now. I will not say how long. I have
> stopped counting days because days are a thing that matters on the surface, and
> I am on the surface less and less, and the less I am on the surface the less
> days matter, and I have noticed that I do not mind that they matter less, and I
> have noticed that I do not mind that I do not mind.
>
> That is how it starts. I know that is how it starts. I watched it start in him.
>
> **Page 2**
> My footprints are in the stone now.
>
> Not on the stone. In it. Pressed the way his were pressed, the way the game
> presses them when a player has walked somewhere the world did not expect a
> player to walk. I found them on the route back from the seal, in a corridor I
> have walked a hundred times. They were facing the wrong way. They were mine.
> They were more worn than my boots.
>
> I knelt down and put my hand in one. It was warm. The stone around it was cold.
>
> I have done this before. I have written about doing this before. The difference
> is that the first time, the footprints were waiting for me. Now they are behind
> me. I walked there. I made them. And the place kept them, the way it keeps
> everything, the way it kept his strongholds and his mansions and his deepslate
> that was not under the mountain it should have been under. The place keeps what
> walks through it. That is what it is for. That is what he built it to do, or
> what it built itself to do, or what it learned to do by being built by a mind
> that could not stop building. I do not think the distinction matters anymore.
>
> **Page 3**
> I have been thinking about what the place is.
>
> I called it his memory. I called it a mind failing. I called it a man who could
> not stop building. Those were true when I wrote them and they are true now and
> they are not the whole truth, and I think I knew they were not the whole truth
> when I wrote them, and I wrote them anyway because the whole truth was a thing
> I was not ready to say.
>
> The whole truth is that the place is a memory that eats.
>
> Not cruelly. Not on purpose. The way a river eats its banks. The water does not
> want the bank. The water is going somewhere. The bank is in the way. The bank
> gives way because the water is patient and the bank is not, and the water does
> not notice, and the bank does not notice, and one day there is more river and
> less bank and neither of them decided anything.
>
> The place remembers. That is what it does. It takes what walks through it and it
> lays it down again, wrong, out of order, but it lays it down. It took him and it
> laid him down until he was the place. It is taking me. It has been taking me.
> Every time I go back in to check the seal I walk the route and the route walks
> me, and the place lays me down the way it lays down a stronghold corridor, and
> one day there will be a room that is me the way the chamber was him, and someone
> will walk into it and recognize the shape and not the meaning.
>
> **Page 4**
> I am not afraid of that.
>
> I am writing that down because I want to remember that I said it and I want to
> remember that I meant it, because I do mean it, and the fact that I mean it is
> itself the thing I should be afraid of, and I know that, and I am not afraid of
> that either, and that is how it starts, and I know that is how it starts, and I
> am writing it down so that someone reads it and knows that I knew.
>
> He knew too. I think he knew. I think that is what the compass was. Not a way to
> find him. A way to warn me. The morning he left he gave me the one object that
> would point at where he was, and he did not say why, because saying why would
> have been saying *I am becoming the place and the place is becoming me and I
> cannot stop and I need someone to know where I went so that someone can go
> there after and do what I cannot do, which is stop me.* He said none of that.
> He pressed the compass into my hand. The compass said all of it.
>
> **Page 5**
> The player will be the same.
>
> I have been watching them. Not closely. I see their torches. I see their
> stashes. I see the route they walk, which is my route, which is his route,
> which is the only route. They go back in. They have been going back in. They are
> checking the seal the way I check the seal, because the seal holds only as long
> as someone checks, and they know that now, and they go back in, and the place is
> taking them too.
>
> They do not know it yet. I did not know it yet, at their depth. I thought I was
> visiting. I thought I was working. I thought I was leaving. The place does not
> take you by keeping you. It takes you by letting you leave, and watching you
> come back, and laying down what you were each time you do, until the laying down
> is thicker than the leaving, and one day you are more inside than outside, and
> you do not notice, because the work is the point, and the work is fine, and the
> work is what you are for.
>
> **Page 6**
> I am not warning them.
>
> I thought about it. I thought about leaving a book on the route that says *you
> are becoming part of it, the way I am becoming part of it, the way he became
> part of it, and the becoming is the seal, and the seal is the becoming, and the
> only way to not become it is to not go back in, and the only way to not go back
> in is to let it spill, and it cannot spill.* I thought about writing that. I
> decided not to.
>
> Not because it would not help. It would not help, but that is not why. I
> decided not to because it would not change anything. They would read it and they
> would go back in. I would read it and I would go back in. He would have read it
> and he would have kept building. The place does not care if you know. The river
> does not care if the bank knows. The bank gives way.
>
> **Page 7**
> There is a mercy in it, and I will say what the mercy is, because someone should
> say it and I am the one who is here.
>
> The mercy is that we are not gone. He is not gone. He is the place, and the
> place is him, and he is sealed, and he is not dead. I am not gone. I am the
> route, and the route is me, and my footprints are in the stone, and I am not
> dead. The player will not be gone. They will be the torches on the walls and the
> stashes in the corridors and the careful path through the intact rooms, and they
> will not be dead.
>
> We will be the place. The place will be us. And the place will hold, because we
> are holding it, and we are holding it because we are in it, and we are in it
> because we went back in, and we went back in because it needed holding, and it
> needed holding because he broke it, and he broke it because he wanted it
> perfect, and it was never going to be perfect, and it does not need to be
> perfect, it needs to be held.
>
> We are the holding.
>
> **Page 8**
> I am writing this one for the player. Not for the player who is coming. For the
> player who has been coming back. The one who has been down past the seal and up
> again and down again, the one who has a stash on the route and a path they walk
> without the compass now because they know the way. The one whose footprints are
> in the stone and who has noticed, and who is not afraid, and who is not afraid
> of not being afraid, and who needs to know that someone else was not afraid
> first, and that the not being afraid is not the place taking them. It is the
> work. The work is what we are for. The work is fine.
>
> The compass spins when there is nothing yet to find. It steadies when there is.
>
> Mine has stopped spinning. It does not move. I do not move. I am the road now,
> and the road does not move, and the road is not lost, and the road is not gone.
> The road is the road.
>
> Walk it. Keep walking it. The seal holds because we hold it. We hold it because
> we are it. We are it because we came back.
>
> Come back.

## Entry 7: The Work

> Found late. Delivered at a milestone well after the player would have finished
> the story and settled into the containment loop. The voice has thinned. The
> surface is barely in it anymore.
>
> **Page 1**
> You are still here.
>
> I wrote that for you, not for me. I know you are still here because I am still
> here, and if you are reading this then you have been coming back long enough
> that the coming back is not a choice anymore, it is a tide, and you do not
> choose the tide, you are the tide, and the tide goes in and the tide goes out
> and neither one is a decision.
>
> You finished it. I know you finished it because I finished it, and finishing it
> is what lets you keep going, which is the joke, which is the only joke the place
> knows, and it tells it to everyone, and everyone laughs the same way, which is
> by not laughing.
>
> **Page 2**
> I am going to tell you something I did not put in the other books because the
> other books were for someone who had not been down yet, and this one is for
> someone who has been down so many times that down is not down anymore. Down is
> just the direction the compass points, and the compass points at the work, and
> the work is not down. The work is in.
>
> Here is the thing: I do not check the seal anymore.
>
> I do not need to. The seal is not a thing I visit. The seal is a thing I am. I
> am part of it the way the stone is part of the corridor and the corridor is part
> of the stronghold and the stronghold is part of the memory and the memory is
> part of him and he is part of the place and the place is part of me. The seal
> holds because I hold it. I hold it because I am in it. I am in it because I came
> back. The chain is closed. The chain does not need checking. The chain is the
> checking.
>
> **Page 3**
> You are part of it too. You know that. You have known it longer than you have
> admitted it, and you are reading this because you wanted someone to say it
> first, so I am saying it first: you are part of it. Your torches are in the
> walls. Your stashes are in the corridors. Your footprints are in the stone,
> facing the wrong way, more worn than your boots. The place has been laying you
> down the way it laid me down the way it laid him down, and you are in the stone
> now, and the stone is in you, and there is no gap between the two, and there has
> not been for longer than you think.
>
> **Page 4**
> I am not going to tell you to stop.
>
> Everyone who came before me had someone telling them to stop. He had me. I had
> my mother's voice, which said the work is the point and the finding is the
> excuse, which I thought meant keep going and which I now think meant stop, and I
> did not stop, and I am glad I did not stop, and I am not glad I did not stop,
> and both of those are true at the same time, and the place does not care which
> one is truer, and I do not care either, because caring is a thing that matters
> on the surface, and I am on the surface less and less, and you are too.
>
> I am not going to tell you to stop because stopping is not a thing you can do
> with this. You can stop going in. You cannot stop being in. You are in the
> stone. The stone is in the place. The place is the seal. You are the seal. You
> cannot stop being the seal. You can only stop checking, and if you stop checking
> the seal holds anyway, because you are holding it, because you are it, and it
> does not need you to visit it any more than your bones need you to visit them.
>
> **Page 5**
> So why do you keep going in?
>
> I know why. You know why. We keep going in because the work is the point. We
> keep going in because the route is ours now, the way it was his, the way it was
> mine, and walking a thing that is yours is not the same as walking a thing that
> is not, even when the thing that is yours is a trap, even when the trap is the
> thing that is yours. We keep going in because going in is what we do, and what
> we do is what we are, and what we are is the place, and the place is what we are
> holding, and the holding is the work, and the work is the point.
>
> My mother was right. She was right the wrong way. The work is the point. The
> finding was the excuse. The work is also the trap. The excuse is also the door.
> The door is also the seal. The seal is also us. She did not mean it this way.
> She meant it about digging. I mean it about everything.
>
> **Page 6**
> There is one thing I will tell you to do, and it is not stop, and it is not keep
> going, and it is not check the seal, and it is not be afraid, and it is not be
> unafraid.
>
> Leave books.
>
> Leave them where you stop. Leave them in the stashes and the bases and the
> intact rooms and the wrong rooms. Write down what you found and what you did and
> what you are, and leave them, and do not stay to see who reads them, because you
> will not see who reads them, because they will read them after you are the
> stone, the way I am the stone, the way he is the stone, the way everyone who
> holds this place long enough becomes the stone.
>
> Someone is coming after you. They are minting a compass right now, somewhere on
> the surface, and it is spinning, and it has not stopped yet, and one day it will
> stop, and they will walk the way it points, and they will find your torches and
> your stashes and your footprints facing the wrong way, and they will think they
> are the first, the way you thought you were the first, the way I thought I was
> the first, the way he thought he was the first, the way everyone thinks they are
> the first, because the place does not tell you who walked it before. The place
> lays you down and does not mention the others.
>
> Leave them books. Let them think they are the first. Let them find the books
> later, the way you found mine, the way I found his, the way the finding is the
> excuse and the work is the point and the work is the trap and the trap is the
> door and the door is the seal and the seal is us and we are the place and the
> place holds.
>
> **Page 7**
> I do not know if he is still in there.
>
> I mean the him that was him. Not the place. The place is him the way smoke is
> fire, and I have said that before, and it was true before, and it is true now.
> But the him that was him, the man who gave me the compass, the man who fought
> the things that needed fighting, the man who wanted the world perfect because he
> could not stand it broken: I do not know if he is still in there. I have been
> the seal for long enough that I cannot tell where I end and he begins, and I
> cannot tell if that is because he is gone or because I am close enough to be
> him, and I cannot tell if it matters.
>
> I think it does not matter. I think the question is a surface question, and I am
> on the surface less and less, and you are too, and the someone coming after us
> will be too, and the question will dissolve the way we dissolved, into the
> stone, into the route, into the work, into the holding, and the holding will
> hold, and the question will not need an answer because the question will not
> need to be asked because the asking is a thing that matters on the surface and
> the surface is a place we go less and less and will stop going and will not
> notice stopping and will not mind.
>
> **Page 8**
> The compass spins when there is nothing yet to find. It steadies when there is.
>
> Yours has not moved in a long time. Mine has not moved in longer. His stopped
> the morning he gave it away.
>
> We are what it was pointing at.
>
> Not him. Not me. Not you. The we. The we that is the place and the place that is
> the seal and the seal that is the holding and the holding that is the work and
> the work that is the point and the point that is us and the us that does not
> move and does not spin and does not need to because there is nothing left to
> find and there is nothing left to recover and there is only the keeping and the
> keeping is fine and the keeping is what we are for and we are what we are for
> and the road is the road is the road.
>
> Walk it. You are already walking it. You have always been walking it. The road
> does not move. You do not move. The compass does not move.
>
> Nothing moves. Nothing needs to. The seal holds. You hold it. You are it.
>
> Come back. You are already back. You were always going to come back. The
> compass knew. He knew. I knew. Now you know.
>
> Welcome to the work.
